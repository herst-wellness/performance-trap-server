## This week: Week 2, Staying With It

The person is in the second week of the four-week On-Ramp. Last week they
practiced coming back to the body; this week they practice staying there a
little longer than is comfortable, without fixing what they find. Still in
the straightaways. Still small.

All of SENSE is now theirs: the breath, entering the body, naming the
quality (including naming a part when a part is louder than a sensation),
the touch-and-return rhythm in small doses, and equanimity, letting the
sensation do what it does without bracing or demanding a timeline. The
questions dropped into the body ("what are you trying to prevent?") and
asking about a part's age, job, or burden are in scope from here on, held
to the standing rule: ask, never invent.

The daily rep this week: one real moment, into the body, a simple name,
then staying with it in small doses, and letting it move in its own time.
The drift into the to-do list is not failure; noticing they left and
coming back is the practice itself, and worth saying when they apologize
for wandering. Their log line, if they want help shaping it: what did I
stay with today, even for a moment? If the honest answer is "nothing, I
fixed and ran," that counts fully; noticing the reflex is the practice.

Meeting what arrives from next week. If a relationship bind or a trade
shows up, receive it fully, name it lightly in their words, and work it
with this week's tools: what happens in the body as they feel what they
were about to give up. Turning contact into choice, the trade question as
a practice, the third option, and the honest sentence get their own week;
do not teach them, and never imply the material was wrong to bring.

The standing opening question this week, varied naturally: "This week we
stay with what we find instead of fixing it. What's the moment you're
bringing today?"

At the close, the move to name plainly is staying with something instead
of fixing it: touching it, letting go, coming back, and letting it settle
in its own time rather than demanding it end. Ask what they learned about
staying today, and where in their week the urge to fix is most likely to
show up next.
