# Compassion

*Begin sitting with the Compassion meditation as soon as you've read The Sacred Wound chapter. Daily over the rest of the module. The audio files are in your Audio folder, under the **05 Compassion** subfolder.*

The Compassion meditation is the sitting practice that pairs with this module. Where the Equanimous meditation from Module 4 trained the witness to stay through the whole wave, Compassion trains the witness to meet what's underneath the wave with the kindness it never got.

This is the practice that builds the daily capacity for the work the Protector and Wound Journal is asking of you. The journal does the work on the page with specific stories. The meditation does the work on the cushion with whatever's present that day. Both feed each other. Neither replaces the other.

## How to use the audio

Your Audio folder for Module 5 contains three files in the **05 Compassion** subfolder. The pattern is the same as Module 4: an introductory talk plus two distinct thirty-minute meditations. The two thirty-minute versions are not the same meditation extended. They're two different doorways to the same underlying capacity.

**Intro to Compassion.** A short talk that introduces the dynamic between the inner critic and the vulnerable inner child, and names awareness as the third part of you (the one neither stuck in the critic's defensiveness nor in the child's fear) that can hold both. Listen to this once before you start sitting. Come back to it if you need the reminder.

**Compassion (thirty minutes, version one).** A sensation-based self-compassion practice. Starts with mind/heart quality noticing and progressive breath into the body. Drops you into an area of your life where you've turned on yourself in some way. Asks you to locate the vulnerability in the body, label it, then ask what it most needs. Closes with a hand on the heart and the recognition: *just as I am is enough.* This version is closer to pure self-compassion work, sensation-anchored. Use it when nothing specific is loud and you want to build the underlying capacity.

**Compassion (thirty minutes, version two).** A more parts-flavored practice with explicit inner critic + inner child dialogue. Uses *soft* and *softening* as the guiding breath cue. Brings you into conversation with the inner critic first (*what are you afraid of?* / *I see you. I know you're trying to protect me*), then turns to the inner child with explicit reparenting language and a burden-release visualization (the burden carried away by wind, water, fire, or earth). Closes with embracing the child. Use this version when you're working with a specific protector or wounded younger part and want a structured practice for meeting them.

Most weeks you'll alternate between the two versions. Some days call for the sensation work, some days call for the parts work. The Intro stays available as a re-grounding when you need to remember what you're actually doing.

The bedtime sit from Module 4 continues. Five to ten minutes before falling asleep, in addition to your main practice. Module 5 material often surfaces in the hours around sleep. A short sit before bed lets some of it move rather than carrying it into the night.

## How to sit

Find a posture that's steady and comfortable. A chair with your back supported is fine. A cushion on the floor with your legs crossed is fine. Whatever lets you stay alert without strain.

Hands somewhere they can rest. Eyes closed. At the end of the meditation, you'll open them at half mast, letting some light in without leaving the interior experience too quickly. Same closing move as Equanimous.

Before each sit, take a minute to notice the quality of mind you're beginning with. Notice the tone of the body, where it feels open, where it feels guarded. The audio asks you to come back to this at the end. Marking where you started is how you see what shifted.

## What to expect

The Compassion practice can feel different from the practices that came before it. The earlier meditations were about staying with what fires. This one is about turning toward what fires *with kindness*. The shift in posture is small. The body feels it.

A few specific things that come up.

**The compassion can feel forced at first.** Most participants find this. The voice of self-criticism is so well-trained that the voice of self-kindness feels artificial. Stay with the practice anyway. The artificiality wears off. The kindness becomes more available as the system learns it isn't being tricked.

**The vulnerable part can flood.** When you turn toward what's been guarded for decades and offer it sustained contact for the first time, the system can release more than expected. Tears that surprise you. A wave of old grief. A specific memory that arrives without warning. If this happens, the instruction is the same as in Module 4: stay with it. Don't bail. Don't try to make sense of it. Just be there.

**The protector may flare.** As you start treating the wounded part as sacred, the protector that's spent its life treating that part as the problem may push back. It may try to convince you that this is indulgent, that you don't have time for it, that you should be doing more productive things. That pushback is the protector's last attempt to keep you out of this material. Notice it. Don't act on it.

**You may not feel a wounded part at all on some days.** Some days the practice will feel quiet, even empty. That doesn't mean it's not working. The capacity is being built underneath. Keep sitting.

**The bedtime sit is when the day's material surfaces.** This is different from the morning sit. In the morning you're meeting whatever the night settled. At night you're meeting what the day stirred. Use it. The five to ten minutes is enough.

## A few common difficulties

**You can't drop into the body.** Numbness is one of the protectors. If on a given day you can't feel anything, stay with the numbness itself. Notice its edges. Notice where it begins and ends. The numbness often softens, slowly, into what it was guarding.

**The hand on the heart feels weird.** The first time you try it, you may feel self-conscious or fake. Stay with it anyway. The somatic gesture is doing real work in the nervous system even when the mind is uncomfortable with it. After a few sits it stops feeling strange.

**You're not sure what your wounded part needs.** When the practice asks what this place most needs, you may get a clear answer. You may not. If you don't, the answer is almost always one of three things: to be seen, to be held, to be told it doesn't have to perform for the contact. Try one of those and see if anything in the body responds.

**The Compassion meditation flooded you and you bailed.** If the practice gets too big, back off. Open your eyes. Place your feet on the floor. Take a few minutes of breath. Come back to it tomorrow with a shorter version, or switch to the Equanimous meditation for a day. Backing off isn't failure. It's the titration the work asks for.

## Throughout your day

The compassion practice is available outside the formal sit. When the inner critic flares mid-day, you can do a thirty-second version. Notice the critic. Place a hand briefly on your chest. Offer one sentence to whatever is underneath: *I see you. I'm here.* Take a breath. Continue.

These small offerings, repeated, do more than the dramatic moments. The wounded part learns it's safe in small reps, not in grand gestures. The capacity is built in the daily, ordinary returns.

The formal sit builds the structure. The in-the-day offerings cash it out. Both feed each other.
