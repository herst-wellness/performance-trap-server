# Heartfelt Intentions

The Welcome said what you value grew out of where you got hurt, and that you uncover it rather than invent it. Here's what I mean, from my own life.

A few years ago, I was ghosted by friends I had considered family. Almost a decade of sitting in ceremony together. The last five years close enough that when their home burned down in the Santa Cruz fires, we took them in, helped them find a new place, and furnished some of it with our own furniture.

Then a few unreturned calls, an odd tone, then silence. I learned from him eventually that they'd cut us off because she had decided Melissa flirted with him. There was never a conversation. One moment we were family and the next we were gone.

That hurt landed in a way that didn't make sense at first, because the story was about adults and a misunderstanding and my body was reacting to something much older.

When I sat with it, what surfaced was being six. I'd asked my dad to tuck me in, which he usually did. He said no, walked into his office and closed the door. I stood outside crying and waiting for footsteps, then went to bed and cried myself to sleep. The next morning my mother asked and I said I was fine. That's the thing I learned. Say you're fine. Close your own door before somebody closes it on you.

And that night became a blueprint. Forty years later friends turned away without a word and the same six-year-old's ache came straight back.

But what I came to see, after sitting with it long enough, is that what hurt most wasn't the ghosting. It's what got violated. The thing that mattered to me, that had always mattered to me, was *being met. Being included in the conversation. Reciprocity.* The wound showed me that with a clarity I'd never had before.

So the reason it hurt as much as it did is that it ran straight against the value my nervous system had been organized around protecting since I was six.

That value, once named, became a heartfelt intention. Reciprocity. Showing up for people the way I want to be shown up for, asking for the conversation when I notice it's missing, and refusing to ghost the people I love no matter how hard the truth is to say.

That's what a heartfelt intention is.

## What a Heartfelt Intention Is

A heartfelt intention isn't a goal and it isn't a to-do, and it isn't something you have to go and acquire. It's a way of being that already lives in you, and it gets revealed when the specifics of the wound show you what mattered to you all along.

A goal is *I want to make six figures this year,* and an intention is *I want to live in line with what I value.*

A to-do is *call my mother on Sunday,* and an intention is *to be present with the people I love, even when it costs me something.*

Goals and to-dos sit on top of intentions, and they're the actions that follow once an intention is clear. The intention underneath is the *who I want to be in the world,* the *what matters to me.* And once you know it, the actions get easier to spot.

So the first half of this module is uncovering yours.

## Why They Live in the Wound

And here's the paradox. Your heartfelt intentions live in the same place as your sacred wound, because the intention is what the wound was guarding the path back to.

Module 5 taught you to meet the wound with compassion. But what I didn't fully say then is that the wound also carries a map. The specifics of what you got hurt around point directly at what you most value. If you were hurt around being unseen, what you value is being met. If it was being controlled, you value freedom. And if it was being abandoned, you value reciprocity, or steadiness, or being shown up for.

The pain wasn't random. It landed where it did because that thing mattered, and the mattering was already in you. The wound only showed where it was.

When my friends ghosted us, the pain didn't tell me I should start caring about reciprocity. It told me I'd always cared about it, and the violation was making that visible in a way ordinary life never had.

So that's the move I'm asking for. Not to invent new values, but to uncover the ones your wound has been guarding the way back to.

## The Story, Sensation, Violation, Longing Arc

The method goes through four stages in order, and each one builds on the one before it. The body leads and the mind follows.

**Story.** You recall a moment of being hurt, and not in the abstract. Who was there, what happened, what it cost you. The body needs the texture of the actual moment before it'll give us anything useful. *I was six. My dad said no when I asked him to tuck me in. He went into his office and closed the door.* That kind of specific.

**Sensation.** You drop into what happens in the body as you remember it. Where you feel it, and what it feels like. Tightness, heat, hollowness, weight. This is where the work leaves the mind and gets into the place that knows, and while the story may be old, the sensation is happening now.

**Violation.** You name what was missing or crossed. Safety, belonging, voice, permission to be yourself, dignity. You find the language for what got taken, and most people have never once said it out loud, which is why saying it is its own relief.

**Longing.** You name what that younger part of you most wanted instead, and the longing is the intention in disguise. *I wanted to be seen. I wanted to know I belonged. I wanted to be allowed to be sad. I wanted someone to come find me.* When you let the longing speak, the value underneath shows itself.

Four stages in order, slow each time, and let the body lead.

## The Five Stories

And I'll ask you to do this with five different memories, each tuned to a different layer of the wounding.

**Story 1: The Wound.** A moment that shaped how you saw yourself. Unseen, unheard, told who to be to be acceptable.

**Story 2: The Burden.** A time life asked you to grow up, hold it all together, set boundaries beyond your years.

**Story 3: The Shadow.** A story where shame, control, or fear shaped your choices. A time you hid, overpowered, or felt powerless.

**Story 4: The Letting Go.** A moment you had to release something you couldn't fix or understand.

**Story 5: The Breaking Away.** A time change felt out of control, when something in you broke from the familiar even at the cost of stability or belonging.

Five different ways in, and each tends to surface a different value. By the time you've done all five, the threads underneath usually start showing themselves.

## What Happens in Our Session

You'll do the five stories before we meet. The sensations, the violations, the longings, all of it. It comes to me on its own.

And in the session we don't invent your intentions from scratch. We pull them out of what you've already written. I'll read back what showed up in the longing column across the five, and we'll start seeing threads. The same word twice. The same shape in different memories. The same value violated from different angles.

That's where the language comes from. We name each one together, looking for the words that land in your body rather than abstract concepts. The phrasing that, when you hear it, feels true.

Most people leave that session with somewhere between five and eight named intentions, and they go into the document I send you afterward alongside your life purpose and what your friends said they see in you.
