# Heartfelt Intentions

A few years ago, I was ghosted by friends I had considered family. Almost a decade of sitting in ceremony together. The last five years close enough that when their home burned down in the Santa Cruz fires, we took them in, helped them find a new place, and furnished some of it with our own furniture.

Then one day a few unreturned calls. An odd tone. Then silence. I eventually learned from him that they had cut us off because she had decided Melissa had flirted with him. There was never a conversation. One moment we were family. The next, we were gone.

That hurt landed in a way that didn't make sense at first. The story was about adults and a misunderstanding. The body was reacting to something much older.

When I sat with it, what surfaced was a memory of being six years old. I had asked my dad to tuck me in, something he usually did every night. He said no, walked into his office, and closed the door. I stood outside crying. I waited for footsteps. Eventually I went to bed and cried myself to sleep. The next morning my mother asked about it, and I told her I was fine. That's what I learned to do. To say I was fine. To close my own door before someone else could close it on me.

That night became a kind of blueprint. Forty years later, friends turned away without a word and the same six-year-old's ache came rushing back.

What I came to see, after sitting with all of it long enough, was that what hurt most wasn't the ghosting itself. What hurt was what got violated. The thing that mattered to me, that had always mattered to me, was *being met. Being included in the conversation. Reciprocity.* The wound showed me that with a clarity I'd never had before.

The reason that ghosting hurt as much as it did was that it ran directly against the value my whole nervous system had been organized around protecting since I was six.

That value, named, became a heartfelt intention. Reciprocity. Showing up for people the way I want to be shown up for. Asking for the conversation when I notice it's missing. Refusing to ghost the people I love, no matter how hard the truth is to say.

That's what a heartfelt intention is.

## What a Heartfelt Intention Is

A heartfelt intention isn't a goal. It isn't a to-do. It isn't something you have to acquire or achieve. It's a way of being that already lives in you, that gets revealed when the wound's specifics show you what mattered most to you all along.

A goal is *I want to make six figures this year.* An intention is *I want to live in alignment with what I value.*

A to-do is *call my mother on Sunday.* An intention is *to be present with the people I love, even when it costs me convenience.*

Goals and to-dos sit on top of intentions. They're the actions that follow when an intention is clear. The intention itself is the underlying *who I want to be in the world,* the *what matters to me,* the *what I'm here for.* Once you know the intention, the actions get easier to recognize.

This module's first half is the work of uncovering yours.

## Why They Live in the Wound

This is the paradox. Your heartfelt intentions live in the same place as your sacred wound. The intention is what the wound was guarding the path back to.

Module 5 taught you to meet the wound with compassion. What we didn't fully say then was that the wound also carries a map. The specifics of what you got hurt around point directly at what you most value. If you were hurt around being unseen, what you value is being met. If you were hurt around being controlled, what you value is freedom. If you were hurt around being abandoned, what you value is reciprocity, or steadiness, or being shown up for.

The pain wasn't random. It landed on something specific because that specific thing mattered. The mattering was already inside you. The wound just revealed where it was.

When my friends ghosted us, the pain didn't tell me reciprocity was something I should start caring about. It told me reciprocity was something I had always cared about, and the violation was making the caring visible in a way ordinary life never had.

That's the move this module asks you to make. Not to invent new values. To uncover the values your wound has been guarding the way to.

## The Story, Sensation, Violation, Longing Arc

The method for uncovering them goes through four stages, in order. Each stage builds on the one before it. The body leads. The mind follows.

**Story.** You recall a moment of being hurt. Not in the abstract, but in the specific. Who was there. What happened. What it cost you. The body needs the texture of the actual moment to give us anything useful. *I was six. My dad said no when I asked him to tuck me in. He went into his office and closed the door.* That kind of specific.

**Sensation.** You drop into what happens in your body as you remember it. Where do you feel it. What does it feel like. Tightness, heat, hollowness, weight. This is where the work moves out of the mind and into the place that actually knows. The story might be old. The sensation is happening now.

**Violation.** You name what was missing or crossed. Safety, belonging, voice, permission to be yourself, recognition, dignity. You find the language for what got taken or denied. Most people have never named the violation out loud. Doing so is its own relief.

**Longing.** You name what that younger part of you most wants to experience instead. The longing is the intention in disguise. *I wanted to be seen. I wanted to know I belonged. I wanted to be allowed to be sad. I wanted someone to come find me.* When you let the longing speak, the value underneath shows itself.

Four stages, in order. Slow each time. Let the body lead.

## The Five Stories

The pre-work asks you to do this with five different memories, each one tuned to a different layer of the wounding.

**Story 1: The Wound.** A moment that shaped how you saw yourself. Unseen, unheard, told who to be to be acceptable.

**Story 2: The Burden.** A time life asked you to grow up, hold it all together, set boundaries beyond your years.

**Story 3: The Shadow.** A story where shame, control, or fear shaped your choices. A time you hid, overpowered, or felt powerless.

**Story 4: The Letting Go.** A moment you had to release something you couldn't fix or understand.

**Story 5: The Breaking Away.** A time change felt out of control, when something in you broke from the familiar even at the cost of stability or belonging.

Five different vectors into the wounding. Each one tends to surface a different value. Together they cover most of the territory. By the time you've done all five, the underlying threads usually start to show themselves.

## What Happens in Our Session

You'll do the five stories before we meet. The sensations, the violations, the longings, all of it. Send the work to me 48 hours before our session.

In the session itself, we don't try to invent your intentions from scratch. We pull them out of what you've already written. I'll read back to you what showed up in the longing column across the five stories. We'll start to see threads. Same word appearing twice. Same shape appearing in different memories. Same value getting violated from different angles.

That's where the language comes from. We name each intention together, finding the words that actually land in your body. Not abstract concepts. The specific phrasing that, when you hear it, feels true.

Most participants leave the session with somewhere between five and eight named intentions. They go into the polished document you'll receive after the session, the artifact of this module's work, along with your life purpose statement and the friends' words about what they see in you.
