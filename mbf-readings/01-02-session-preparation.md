# Session Preparation

Welcome gives you the orientation. What follows is the working container: how often we meet, what a session looks like, what I'm asking of you, what you can ask of me. None of this is the work itself, but the work happens inside of it. When the container is clear, the work has room.

## How often we meet, and for how long

We meet every two weeks for 90 minutes, on Zoom. Find a quiet, private place where you can speak freely. A solid Wi-Fi connection matters. I'll start and end on time. If I'm ever a few minutes late, it's because I'm wrapping up with someone else. I won't be more than five.

Stay in rhythm. The work builds over time, and when too much space opens up between sessions, things drift. Every now and then three weeks between meetings is fine. Mostly, hold the every-two-week pace.

## What a session actually looks like

We usually start with a few minutes of breath. It's a way to step out of the noise and back into your body. Most of us never give ourselves that kind of transition between one thing and the next. When we do, we arrive clearer, less defended, more able to feel what's actually here.

From there, we work with whatever's most alive. Often that's what came up in your journaling. Sometimes it's something in your body. Sometimes it's a conversation from the week, a moment of activation, an ache you can't quite shake.

Then we apply a framework to it. Each module gives you a lens: beginner's mind, awareness, kids on the bus (the different parts of you), and a few more as we go. The framework gives you a way of seeing what you couldn't see when you were inside it. The work doesn't stay theoretical for long. We move through inquiry, meaning I ask questions, we explore together, and you try the framework on against the real situation you brought. The theory becomes practical fast.

## I don't need the whole story

I'll often interrupt you. Not because I'm not listening, but because the whole story sometimes circles the truth without landing on it. You might hear me say, "Can you bottom-line that?" or "What are you actually trying to tell me?" I'm not rushing. I'm helping you name the heart of what's going on underneath the explanation.

I'll also redirect you back to your body, often. This isn't a tic. The body holds information the head can't quite get to: the gut sense, the chest tightening, the throat going dry mid-sentence. Part of what this program is teaching you is how to feel what's there and let it inform what you do next.

Sometimes I'll offer a phrase, or an image, a sense of what seems to be moving underneath what you're saying. I won't always be precise. What I'm offering is a starting point. Your job is to check it in your own body and let me know whether it lands. If it doesn't fit, push back and say what fits better. That back-and-forth is how you start to develop your own vocabulary for what's actually happening inside.

Most of what surprises clients about this work is how much their body has been saying that they weren't able to hear. The ability to listen to it, to your own intuition, to the places that are frozen or stuck or quietly aching, is one of the most lasting things you leave with.

## What I'll be asking you to bring

Reading and journaling between sessions matter. The journals sit alongside these readings, one page each, and you write straight into the page by typing or by talking. Your work saves as you go, so you can start on a laptop and finish on a phone, and it reaches me when you press Finished. Nothing to download, nothing to attach. I'd like each journal done at least two days before we meet so I can sit with it and come prepared.

If your journal comes in late, I might not be able to give it the presence it deserves. That's not a punishment. It's just true.

One thing I'll ask more than once: please don't cancel a session because you haven't done the work. Come anyway. If what I'm asking feels like too much, tell me. I can help you focus on what matters most: which journal entries to prioritize, which practices to commit to. Resistance is often where something real is trying to come through, and showing up while you're inside it is part of the work.

## Insights need time

Sometimes a session reveals something powerful. You'll see a pattern you've been living inside for years, and that clarity will make you want to act quickly. Quit. Leave. Restructure. End something.

Give it room first. Especially when it touches a big part of your life: your job, your work relationships, your marriage. Let it breathe for a week or two. Let your body catch up with what your mind has just seen. Insight isn't the same as integration. What feels obvious in the moment sometimes still needs time to land.

## Closing each session

Toward the end, I'll ask something like, "What are you leaving with?" or "What's one thing from today you want to hold onto?" That isn't a wrap-up move. It's a piece of the work called integration. Naming what surfaced helps your system keep it.

After we meet, there's a Follow-Up journal waiting in that module. Do it within 24 to 48 hours. It doesn't need to be long or polished, but it does need to be honest. Writing down what you took from our time helps you stay close to what surfaced.

## Cancellations

I ask for 48 hours' notice if you need to cancel or reschedule. The one exception: if you're sick. A cold, the flu, anything where your body's busy fighting an infection, tell me and we'll reschedule. The work falls flat when your body is offline.

Last-minute cancellations are charged the full fee. I've been doing this long enough not to apologize for that. My time is my service, and I'm asking you to treat it that way.

## The eight-month window

We complete the program within eight months of your start date. Eight months gives us room to absorb the normal interruptions of life without losing momentum: a trip, a family thing, a busy stretch at work. After eight months, if there's been a pattern of canceling or delaying, unused sessions aren't refunded.

## Between sessions

If something comes up between sessions, a win, a loss, something you need to voice, feel free to text me. I prefer text over email. I may not reply immediately, but I'll read what you send. Allow up to 48 hours for a response. If there's a delay, it isn't personal. I'm just busy and will get back to you.

If something cracks open unexpectedly, or a moment of crisis lands and you need more than the regular rhythm, reach out. We can schedule an additional session.

## What I bring, and what I hold

I'll be honest and present, even when what needs to be said isn't easy. I'll share from my own life when it's useful to your work, not otherwise. Ask me anything. I don't hide behind expertise.

I care deeply about this work and about the people I work with. I also won't coddle. My job isn't to make you comfortable. It's to help you see what's been hard to face, and to learn how to stay with yourself, so you stop abandoning the parts of you that most need your attention. If something I say doesn't land, push back. If I miss something, say so. That's how the space stays honest.

Everything you share with me stays with me. I won't reference it, repeat it, or disclose it without your consent. That's not only an ethical line. It's the trust the work runs on.

And I keep my caseload small. I take only as many clients as I can hold well, which means when we're in a session, my attention is on you, not on the next thing.

That's the container. We start there.
