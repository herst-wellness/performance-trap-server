# Be With

*Begin sitting with the Be With meditation as soon as you've read the Be With chapter. Daily over the rest of the module. The audio files are in your Audio folder, under the **03 Be With** subfolder.*

The Be With meditation is the sitting practice that pairs with this chapter. Where the Be With Journal walks you through the practice in writing with one specific moment, this meditation walks you through the same work as a daily sit, with my voice guiding you.

The meditation builds in three stages. The first stage gathers your attention through the breath. The second stage turns your attention into the body, scanning for what's already there. The third stage moves into the actual Be With practice. You meet whatever you find with the titration rhythm: touch, let go, return.

## How to use the audio

Your Audio folder for Module 3 contains two guided meditations in the **03 Be With** subfolder.

- **Be With.** The primary version, around twenty minutes. Start here.
- **Be With (longer).** The same meditation extended, around twenty-five minutes. Use this once you've sat with the shorter version for a few days and want to deepen.

The Aware meditation from Module 2 and the breath practice from Module 1 are still in your hands. If twenty minutes feels like too much on a given day, sit with one of those instead. The point is to sit daily, not to do the hardest version every time.

## How to sit

Find a steady, comfortable posture you can stay in for the length of the meditation. A chair with your back supported is fine. A cushion on the floor with your legs crossed is fine. Whatever lets you stay alert without strain.

Hands somewhere they can rest. Eyes closed, or open at half-mast with a soft gaze just in front of you.

One thing different about this practice: it's worth taking a minute before you start the audio to remember a recent moment that's still got some charge on it. The fight with the partner. The slight at work. The text that landed wrong. You don't need to bring it back vividly. Just have it in mind so the body has something specific to work with when the meditation drops you in.

## What to expect

The first time you sit with this, the protector will probably show up. The mind speeds up. You notice you've started planning the rest of your day. You go a little blank. You feel the urge to check your phone. You think, *this isn't working*.

All of that is the practice working. The protector is the first thing the practice meets. It's doing its job. It's trying to keep you from feeling what's underneath. Your job, in the meditation, is to notice the protector and to bring the titration rhythm to it. Touch, let go, return.

You'll start with the breath. Slow inhalation, longer exhalation. After a few minutes, when the system has settled some, my voice will move you into the body. You'll scan for what's there. A tight chest. A pressure in the throat. Heat in the face. A heaviness in the gut. Whatever you find, you bring your attention to it for a short window.

Then I'll move you back to the breath, or to your hands, or to the sound of the room. You rest there for a window. Then I'll bring you back to the sensation.

That rhythm continues for the rest of the meditation. Touch, let go, return. Touch, let go, return. Each pass, the protector has a chance to recognize that you aren't going to flood it. That recognition is what allows it to loosen.

By the end of the meditation, you may notice the system has softened on its own. You may notice the protector still holding. You may notice the tender thing underneath has surfaced briefly. Any of these are fine. There's nothing you're supposed to feel. The practice is the rhythm, not the outcome.

## A few things that come up

**It feels like nothing's happening.** That's common, especially early. The protector's job is to make the practice feel pointless so you'll stop. Keep sitting. The shift is usually slower than you'd like.

**It feels like too much.** That's the other direction. You touched too hard, stayed too long. Back off sooner next time. Smaller doses. The practice rewards rhythm, not endurance.

**A part of you starts talking.** A part might surface as a voice during the sit. *I shouldn't be doing this. I look stupid. What's the point.* That's the inner critic or another kid. You can use what you learned in Module 2. Notice the part. Name it. Don't argue with it. Come back to the body.

**You get emotional.** Sometimes the protector lets go and the tender thing comes up fast. Tears, grief, an old ache. Let it come. You don't have to do anything with it. The body knows how to release. Your job is to keep yourself company while it happens.

**You fall asleep.** It happens. If it's chronic, sit somewhere more alert (chair instead of bed) or sit at a different time of day. Drowsiness is also sometimes how the protector deflects. If you're drowsy every time, that's information.

## Throughout your day

The same rhythm is available to you outside formal sitting. When something fires in your body in the middle of your day, the meeting with your partner, the email that landed wrong, the slight at the school pickup, you can do a thirty-second version of this practice in real time. Notice the sensation. Stay with it for a moment. Let your attention shift to your breath or your hands. Come back to the sensation. Let go. Come back.

You're not trying to fix anything. You're letting the system know you're not going to override it this time. Over weeks, that builds a different default.

The daily sit is what makes the in-the-moment version available. The capacity gets built on the cushion. It gets cashed out in the world.
