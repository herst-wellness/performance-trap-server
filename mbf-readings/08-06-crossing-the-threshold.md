# Crossing the Threshold: A Closing Ritual

The last reading asked you to be the change in something larger than your own life. This one ends the program, because you've been going down for a long stretch and the last act of any journey is coming back up and across.

In every old myth the threshold between the underworld and the ordinary world gets marked. Not walked through. Marked. Something is poured out, something left behind, certain words said, a fire lit, a name taken. And I don't think the marking is decoration. It's how a journey gets sealed into the body and into memory, into the part of you that holds its shape after this ends.

We don't have many threshold rituals left in modern life. Graduations gesture at one. Weddings, the few that still feel real. Funerals, sometimes. Most of the important transitions we make get no acknowledgment at all. We finish therapy and just stop showing up. We end relationships with a text. We complete years of inner work and quietly close the laptop on the last call.

## Don't do that here.

This piece is a simple ritual to mark the end of Mind/Body Foundations. You can do it the day of our final session or the day before. It takes about thirty minutes. You do it alone.

### What you'll need

- A quiet place where you won't be interrupted.
- A candle.
- A small notebook or piece of paper.
- A pen.
- One object that, for you, represents what you're leaving behind. (It can be anything. A worry stone, a scrap of paper with a sentence on it, an old photograph, a key, a stress ball. The point is that it carries weight for you.)
- One object that, for you, represents what you're carrying forward. (Same rule. Anything that means something. A small stone, a piece of jewelry, a written word, a flower, a feather.)

## The ritual

### 1. Settle (5 minutes)

Sit. Light the candle. Close your eyes. Take fifteen slow breaths. Don't try to make them special. Just count them. Let yourself land in the room.

When you feel settled, open your eyes and place both objects in front of you, side by side.

### 2. Naming what you came in with (10 minutes)

Pick up the object that represents what you're leaving behind.

Hold it. Feel its weight in your hand.

Out loud or in your head, name the things you came into this work carrying. The patterns. The fears. The version of yourself you were exhausted by. The story about who you were that you were ready to put down. Be specific. Use real names if real names belong here.

When you've named what needs to be named, write it on a piece of paper. As short or as long as you need. The page is for you. No one else will read it.

When you're done, place the paper under the object. Set them aside.

### 3. Naming what you're carrying forward (10 minutes)

Pick up the second object.

Hold it the same way.

Now name what you're taking with you. Not aspirations. Capacities. The things that have actually shifted in you. The new ways you've learned to meet yourself. Your heartfelt intentions and life purpose statements, in your own current words. Speak them.

Then name your commitments for the next chapter. Specific ones. The daily practice. The three people you've invited in. The support you've chosen for what's ahead: on your own, a rhythm of check-ins, or continued work with me. Say that choice out loud. It counts differently when the body hears it. The first thirty days. The longer arc.

Write these on a separate piece of paper. Keep this one. Fold it small. Put it somewhere you'll find it again. A wallet, a journal, a drawer you open often.

### 4. The vow (5 minutes)

Put the second object back in front of you.

Say, out loud, in whatever language feels honest:

*I have done this work. I will continue to do this work. I will tend what has begun to grow in me. I will not turn away.*

Or your own version. The exact words don't matter. What matters is that you say them out loud, alone, with the candle lit, and that you mean them.

Sit for another minute or two with what you've just said.

Then blow out the candle.

## Afterwards

Take the first object, the one that represents what you're leaving, and dispose of it in whatever way feels right. Bury it in the garden. Drop it in a moving body of water. Throw it away. Burn the paper. The specifics don't matter. The action of letting it go does.

Keep the second object somewhere you'll see it. Not in storage. In view.

Bring whatever came up in the ritual to our final session if it feels useful. Or don't. The ritual was for you, not for me.

## A note on doing this seriously

I know this kind of thing can feel uncomfortable for some people. Performative, maybe. Or too woo, or too earnest, or too much. If that's where you are, I'd ask you to do it anyway.

There's something the old traditions understood about ritual that we've largely forgotten. The body and the deeper layers of the mind don't take changes seriously until they're enacted. You can know intellectually that you're done with a chapter, and the part of you that runs your daily reactions can still be operating as if nothing has changed. Ritual is what tells those deeper layers that we are not where we were. The new thing has begun.

Skip this, and you'll likely do fine. Mind/Body Foundations isn't going to be undone by the absence of a candle. But the people I've worked with who really integrate this work, who are still living it five and ten years later, almost all marked the ending in some way that mattered to them. So I'm asking.

Mark the threshold. Then walk across.
