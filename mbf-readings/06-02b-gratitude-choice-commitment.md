# Gratitude-Choice-Commitment

*Begin sitting with Gratitude-Choice-Commitment as soon as you've read this doc. Daily over the rest of the module. The audio files are in your Audio folder, under the **06 Gratitude** subfolder.*

Gratitude-Choice-Commitment is the sitting practice that pairs with this module. Where the Compassion meditation from Module 5 trained the witness to meet what's underneath the wave with kindness, GCC turns the witness toward three specific moves in a single arc: opening to what's been given, releasing what no longer expresses you, and committing to what you actually want to offer.

What this practice does is build the daily capacity for the work the Heartfelt Intentions and Life Purpose process is asking of you. The pre-work and the session pull your values and gifts into language. This sit asks you to live from them, one day at a time.

It's also tune in to the trade in cushion form. The Choice and Commitment movements explicitly ask: what am I trading away that no longer expresses who I am, and what am I committing to honoring instead. You'll meet the same question in the Follow-Up Journal after our session, in slower form on the page. Daily and post-session, the practice has two homes.

## How to use the audio

Your Audio folder for Module 6 contains four files in the **06 Gratitude** subfolder. The pattern is similar to Module 5: an intro talk plus the main practice in multiple shapes.

**Intro to Module 6.** A short talk that frames the work of this module. The ego as an accumulated set of beliefs from family and culture about what's valuable, the slow process of unpeeling those layers, the essence underneath that's unique to you. Listen to this once at the start. Come back to it whenever you need the reminder of what you're actually uncovering.

**Gratitude (shorter standalone).** A gratitude-focused sit, opening the chest to what's been given. Good as a gateway into the practice and as the shorter option on days when the full GCC sit isn't possible. Connects you to the gift of the day, the people who see you, the body, the teachers, your own willingness. Use this when time is short or when the heart needs to soften before the rest of the work can land.

**Gratitude-Choice-Commitment (thirty minutes, detailed).** The full version with metta phrases (*may I be happy, may I be safe, may I be well, may I be free from suffering*) and the softening cue (*soft, soft, soft*). Starts with the four-part breath into the body, scans the body, locates the strongest sensation, drops in with metta, and then moves into the inquiry sequence: what to release, what's inspiring you, what to commit to, what gift to offer. Use this when you want the loving-kindness layer wrapped around the choice-and-commitment work.

**Gratitude-Choice-Commitment (thirty minutes, standard).** Same sit, stripped of the metta phrases. More streamlined. Use this on days when you want the inquiry without the loving-kindness layer, or when the metta is starting to feel rote.

Most days, alternate between the two thirty-minute versions. The shorter Gratitude sit is your fallback for tight days. The Intro stays available as a re-grounding when you need it.

**The bedtime sit from Module 4 continues.** Five to ten minutes before falling asleep, in addition to your morning practice. Module 6 material, especially the inquiry around what to let go of and what to commit to, often surfaces in the hours around sleep. A short sit before bed lets it move rather than carrying it into the night.

## How to sit

Find a posture that's steady and comfortable. A chair with your back supported is fine. A cushion on the floor with your legs crossed is fine. Whatever lets you stay alert without strain.

Hands somewhere they can rest. Eyes closed for most of the sit. At the end, you'll open them at half-mast, letting some light in without leaving the interior experience too quickly. Same closing move as Equanimous and Compassion.

Before each sit, take a minute to notice the quality of mind you're beginning with. The audio asks you to come back to this at the end. Marking where you started is how you see what shifted.

## What to expect

The GCC practice has three movements inside one sit. Each does something different. Worth knowing what each is doing so you can stay with it when something gets uncomfortable.

**Gratitude opens the chest.** The first movement asks you to connect to what's been given to you. The breath, the body, the people in your life, the simple gifts you usually overlook. Some days this lands easily. Some days the heart feels closed and the gratitude sounds hollow. When it feels forced, stay with the forcing. The act of trying to find one true thing to be grateful for is its own practice. Eventually one lands. The chest softens. Then the rest of the sit becomes possible.

**Choice asks what's worth letting go of.** The second movement asks you to name one thing you could release today that no longer expresses who you actually are. A judgment of yourself. A grievance you've been carrying. A commitment that doesn't fit anymore. This is where the protector often flares. The protector has organized your life around holding on to certain things, and being asked to release one of them can feel destabilizing. Notice the flare. Don't act on it. Just name the thing the practice is pointing at and see what happens when you let yourself say it, even silently.

**Commitment asks what's worth honoring.** The third movement asks what you'd commit to today that expresses what you care about and who you are. What you'd offer in service of others. This is where your heartfelt intentions show up most directly in the practice. As you move through this module, the answers here will start tracking your named intentions. Before the intentions are named, the answers point at them.

A few things that come up in this practice.

**The inquiry questions can feel impossible to answer.** *What's one judgment I could release today?* The mind goes blank. That's fine. Let the question be an invitation, not an assignment. Sit with the question and see what arrives. If nothing arrives, the sitting is the practice. Tomorrow's sit may surface what today's didn't.

**The commitment can feel performative.** Some days the answer to *what could I commit to today* sounds like something you're supposed to say. Notice that. Try the question again. The real answer is usually quieter, and often slightly inconvenient.

**The metta phrases can feel mechanical at first.** *May I be happy. May I be safe. May I be well.* If the words feel hollow, stay with them anyway. The phrasing is doing somatic work even when the mind is uncomfortable with it. After a few sits, the phrases start to land.

**The Choice movement can surface old trades.** Once you start practicing the noticing of what no longer expresses you, you'll see trades you've been making for years. A standing arrangement that doesn't fit. A way you talk about yourself that you outgrew. A relationship dynamic that's been costing you. Don't try to fix any of it from the cushion. Just see it. The fixing comes later, in conversation.

**Some sits will feel quiet.** Not every sit will produce a vivid release or commitment. Some days the practice is just sitting with the breath and the body. That's still the practice. The capacity is being built even when nothing dramatic happens.

## A few common difficulties

**Numbness in the gratitude movement.** Some days nothing feels worth being grateful for. The system is too tired or too clenched. The instruction is the same as in earlier modules: stay with what's there. Notice the numbness. Let it be the starting point. Often the gratitude finds its way in through a side door once you stop forcing it.

**The protector trying to talk you out of the sit.** *You don't have time for this. Nothing important is going to come up today.* That's the protector flaring before the Choice movement asks it to release something. Notice the voice. Sit anyway.

**Trying to make the inquiry produce results.** The questions aren't designed to extract answers. They're designed to open the territory where the answers live. If you find yourself trying to engineer the right intention or the right release, stop trying and come back to the breath. The answer arrives or it doesn't. Either way the sit is doing its work.

**The bedtime sit feels different.** It is. In the morning you're meeting whatever the night settled. At night you're meeting what the day stirred. Five to ten minutes is enough. The point isn't to process every charge. It's to let some of it move before sleep.

## Throughout your day

The GCC questions are useful outside the formal sit. Mid-day, when you notice a charge or a choice point, you can ask one of them: *what am I being asked to release here? What would I commit to in this moment if I let myself? Whose gift could I offer right now?*

These small applications, repeated, do as much as the formal practice. The cushion builds the structure. The in-the-day moments cash it out. Both feed each other.

The formal sit also pairs with the in-session work. The intentions we'll name together, the purpose statements your friends help us see, all of it gets practiced from the cushion outward.
