# Welcome

You can name the kids. You can catch the critic mid-sentence and hear it as a voice rather than a verdict, and you've probably had at least one moment of meeting a part instead of fighting it.

So here's what I expect is also happening, and I want to say it before you tell me.

You can see it, and you still can't stop it.

Something fires. The throat clamps or the gut drops or the heat comes up the back of the neck, and by the time you've noticed, the kid is already driving and the thing has already come out of your mouth. You can name what happened afterward with real precision. In the moment you had nothing.

That isn't the work failing. It's where the work goes next.

## Seeing Isn't Stopping

Awareness bought you a gap. What you're finding is that the gap opens slightly too late.

That's because the part isn't the first thing that happens. Something fires underneath it, faster than thought, and the part climbs into the seat afterward to handle what's already started. Module 2 met the characters on the bus. This module is about the engine that starts them up.

And I want to be straight with you about the stretch you're heading into, because nobody told me about it and I lost years to the confusion.

I could explain my own pattern by my late twenties. I knew I'd been the good kid, I knew what it had cost, I could describe the whole machinery to anyone who asked, and I was fluent about it. And I went on running it for another twenty years. The explanation was excellent and it changed nothing. For a long time I took that as evidence that either the understanding was wrong or I was.

It was neither. There's a period in this work where you can see the pattern perfectly and do absolutely nothing about it. You'll describe it back to me well. You'll sound like someone who's got it. Then you'll go home and it'll run exactly as it always has.

That's not a sign it isn't working. It's the normal shape of learning something that changes you rather than something you add on, and seeing always arrives before the ability to do anything with it. The gap between them is uncomfortable and it can last weeks. When you're in it, tell me. Don't wait until you're out of it to mention it, because that stretch is usually where the work is actually happening and it's much easier with someone else in the room.

## The Loop Underneath

The thing that fires has a name. In Buddhist psychology it's called a *sankhara*, and I use the word because English doesn't have a clean one.

A sankhara is a groove. A pattern worn so deep by repetition that the system runs it without consulting you, the way a line carved into wet concrete decides where the water goes once it's set. Same trigger, same groove, every time.

It has a structure, and that's the good news, because a structure has places in it. Something lands. The body responds before you've decided anything. A story forms to explain the body. A reaction follows the story. And then there's the aftermath, the shame or the resentment or the exhaustion that hangs around afterward.

Most people try to interrupt that sequence at the story, by arguing with their thoughts, or at the reaction, by forcing themselves to behave differently. I've watched a lot of intelligent people work very hard at both. Both are too late. By the story, the groove is already running.

The opening is earlier than either. It sits at the body's first response, ahead of any story, and it's small and quiet and easy to miss.

## Staying

Which brings me to what I'm actually going to ask of you here, and it's the least intuitive thing in the program so far.

Stay with it.

Not analyze it. Not fix it. Not breathe it away. Stay with the sensation at the moment it arrives, before the mind has decided what it means, and keep it company while it does whatever it does.

Everything in you will want to do something else. Most of my clients have spent their whole lives running from exactly this: overriding it, outworking it, performing past it. And the patterns got stronger, not weaker, because a groove that never gets felt never gets anything but deeper.

I'll tell you what convinced me, since I resisted this for years. The thing I was most sure I couldn't survive feeling turned out to be smaller than the effort of not feeling it. Not pleasant. Smaller. I had spent decades building a life around avoiding something that took about four minutes to actually meet.

And there's something underneath the staying that I'd rather you found than took from me. Every time you stay with what you'd normally run from, you make a small return to the person you were before you learned to contort yourself to belong. That work doesn't finish here. It opens the door.

## Before You Put This Down

Look away and see how many of these you can say back. Getting them wrong is fine and still does the work.

- Seeing a pattern and being able to stop it are separated by more time than anyone expects. That gap is normal and it is not evidence about you.
- The part isn't first. Something fires underneath it, and the part climbs in afterward.
- A sankhara is a groove worn deep enough that the system runs it without consulting you.
- Arguing with the story and forcing the behavior both come too late.
- The opening is at the body's first response, and the move is to stay.
