# Breath Practice

*This is your daily commitment. Ten to fifteen minutes a day, every day, starting today. You'll get a short text from me each morning asking yay or nay. No shame either way. Just data.*

For the next several days and the rest of this module, you'll be working with a daily breathing practice at a 5/7 ratio: a five-count inhale through the nose, a seven-count exhale through pursed lips. Plan to sit for ten to fifteen minutes. If you're just starting, five minutes today is fine. Build up over the first week.

The first five minutes are mostly the body settling. That's the beginning of the shift from sympathetic activation into parasympathetic rest. The next ten minutes are where the work becomes available, where curiosity, presence, and the capacity to feel what's actually here begin to come online.

## Setting up the app

There's a free app called **Allos** that handles the timing for you. I recommend using it for daily practice. Here's how to set it up:

1. On your phone, go to the App Store and search for **Allos** (A-L-L-O-S). Download it.
2. Open the app.
3. In the top panel, set the breathing rate to **5 breaths per minute**.
4. Just below that, use the toggle to change from "equal" to **longer exhale**.
5. Press **Done**.
6. Set the session length to **10 minutes** or **15 minutes**.
7. You can choose any sound or image you like, but I'd encourage you to close your eyes and listen to the sound rather than watch the image. The eyes pull attention outward. Closing them keeps the attention inside.
8. Press **Start Session**.

The app will guide the inhale and exhale with audio cues. You don't have to count.

## How to sit

Sit in a chair with your back upright but not stiff. Feet flat on the floor. Hands resting in your lap. Eyes closed.

If you'd rather sit on the floor or a cushion, fine. The body just needs to be alert and at ease. Avoid lying down. The body will read it as sleep.

## What to expect

The first few sessions may feel awkward, or boring, or like nothing is happening. That's normal. The settling is slow. The mind will wander. When you notice it has, just return to the breath. The returning is the practice.

After a week or two of daily sitting, you'll start to notice the shift more clearly. A slightly different state in the body. A little more room around your thoughts. A capacity to feel what's there without immediately wanting to fix it.

That's what we'll work from in the rest of the program.

If the mind wandering is making you feel like you're failing, [listen to *Puppy Training*](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/qxq62odqrp1xzrn3xga7h/Puppy-Training.mp3?rlkey=k0vb3f20xkmdzw589nmz5oru2&st=yyv1w1pj&dl=0). It's a short talk that reframes the wandering, drawing on Jack Kornfield's puppy training metaphor. The drift and the return is the practice. Self-judgment is not part of the work.

## Throughout your day

Outside of formal practice, return to the breath whenever you think of it. Three deliberate slow breaths before a meeting. Before a hard email. On the way into a conversation you've been avoiding. Each return strengthens your nervous system's ability to find its own balance.

You don't need an app for this. Just slow down the inhale, lengthen the exhale.
