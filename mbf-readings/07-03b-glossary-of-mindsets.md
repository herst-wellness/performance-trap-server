# Glossary of Mindsets

The **Expand Options Journal** asks you to try out different mindsets as ways of reframing a stuck situation. This is the working list. It's a subset of the mindsets we've explored across the training, narrowed to the ones most likely to shift a body that's locked into one view.

Read the list before you start the journal. Notice which ones you respond to. Two or three will probably feel alive. Those are the ones to use.

## Beginner's Mind

The willingness to set aside what you think you know, so something new can come in. Beginner's Mind asks: *What if I don't already understand this? What if I'm missing something obvious because I'm sure I'm not?* Useful when you're certain you're right, or certain about what someone else is going to do.

## Curiosity Over Certainty

A close cousin of Beginner's Mind. Where certainty closes the door, curiosity opens it. The reframing question: *what else might be happening here?* Useful when you've already drawn a conclusion and the conclusion is keeping you stuck.

## Equanimity

The capacity to stay steady in the presence of strong feelings. Not detachment. Not "above it all." Just the willingness to feel what's here without needing it to be different. Useful when reactivity is so high that no other mindset can land.

## Impermanence

The recognition that everything changes, including this. The thing you're certain will define your life forever almost certainly won't. Useful when you've collapsed into thinking the current state is permanent, or when the thing you're afraid of losing was never going to last anyway.

## Self-Compassion

The mindset of speaking to yourself the way you'd speak to someone you love. Most of us run an internal voice we'd never use on a friend. Self-compassion notices the harshness and softens it. Useful when the Inner Critic has taken over the room.

## Inner Critic

The internal voice whose job is to keep you safe by keeping you small. It uses judgment, catastrophizing, and shame to maintain the status quo. The mindset isn't the Critic itself. It's the move of recognizing the Critic as one voice among many. Useful when you're about to act on what feels like an obvious truth and it's actually old protective speech.

## Sacred Wound

The early hurt that, when you turn toward it instead of away, becomes a source of insight rather than just pain. The mindset asks: *what if this place that hurts isn't a defect but a teacher?* Useful when you keep hitting the same wall and the wall starts to feel personal.

## Window of Presence

The range in which you can think and feel and respond clearly. Outside the window, you're hyperaroused or shut down. The mindset asks: *Am I currently inside or outside my window?* If outside, the move isn't to push through. The move is to come back. Useful as a quick diagnostic before any hard conversation.

## Reaction vs. Response

The recognition that there's a difference between what you do automatically and what you choose. The mindset asks: *am I reacting from old conditioning or responding to what's actually here?* Useful when you notice yourself running a script you've run before.

## The Inner Board of Trustees

Your heartfelt intentions and life purpose statements function as a board you can consult under pressure. When the binary collapses your world to two options, the board asks: *which of these values needs to speak here? What boundary preserves dignity? What would steady leadership look like in this moment?* Useful when conflict is loud and the protector wants the wheel.

## Heartfelt Intentions and Life Purpose as Frames

Your heartfelt intentions and your life purpose are also mindsets, in the practical sense. Each one is a lens through which any stuck situation looks different. The question isn't just *what should I do here?* It's *what would honor what I said matters to me.* Often, the move is right there, waiting for you to look in that direction.

## How to use the list

When you're stuck, pick two mindsets that feel different from each other. Try the situation through each one. Notice what your body does with each frame.

You're not looking for the right answer. You're looking for the frame that opens something that was closed. The body is the test. If the frame doesn't shift anything inside you, it's not the right frame yet. Try another.
