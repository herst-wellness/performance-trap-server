# Surfing an Emotion

*This is **E** in **SENSE**: equanimity. The fifth move, and the close of the inner half.*

*There's [a recording of this one](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/zy4avqa9lgsndhxjtcrz4/Surfing-an-Emotion.mp3?rlkey=ri89bdfb905ytrhl4k3lf5hss&st=vg4b33dy&dl=0) in my own voice, walking the practice through step by step.*

The last chapter said the string fades on its own if you stop plucking it. Which leaves the practical question, and it's the one I get asked in every Module 4 session: what am I supposed to do while it's still loud?

That's this chapter.

The surfing image isn't mine. It comes from the psychologist Raphael Kushner, and what I like about it is that a surfer riding a wave never says *that's a bad wave*, because the wave they're on is the wave. So I'm not asking you to judge the feeling tone that's arrived. I'm teaching you to ride it.

The surfboard is your awareness and the wave is what's moving underneath the story. And the shore is where it puts you down, in a different state from the one it picked you up in.

## Energy in Motion

The word *emotion* comes from *emovere*, to move out. Emotions are energy in motion, and when the energy is allowed to move it runs its cycle and it's done, and when it's blocked it stays.

And that stuckness is where I think most of our chronic difficulties come from. The headaches, the insomnia, the shortness with the people we love. The energy was never the problem for any client I've had. The blocking of it was.

So I'm not asking you to make the emotion happen, and I'm not asking you to make it go away. I'm asking you to stay out of its way long enough that it can finish.

## The Three Doors That Don't Work

When a feeling arrives I do one of three things, and so do you.

I resist. I brace against it, tighten the jaw, hold the breath, clamp the gut. That's meant to make it smaller and it doesn't, because all it does is keep the thing pressed exactly where it is.

And I distract. The phone, the drink, the spreadsheet, the text to a friend. That works for about three minutes, and the feeling is still there when I come back, usually louder, because nothing got metabolized.

I discharge. I throw it at somebody. I blame, I pick the fight, I send the email. The pressure does drop for a moment, and it costs me a relationship or my self-respect, and by tomorrow I'm back where I started with an apology to make.

I had a fourth-door failure on a video call during the lockdowns, with a senior executive who was panicking about layoffs. I was the calm one, the one he could trust. Inside I hadn't slept in days, my gut was twisting, my jaw was clenched and my ears were ringing. Then he stopped and asked, almost offhand, whether everything was all right over there. And for about half a second I saw the option to tell him the truth. I said, *yeah, just a rough night.* The trade didn't happen in the lie. It happened in the half second before it, when the old thing was faster.

Resist, distract, discharge. None of the three gets you to the other side, and surfing is the fourth door. You stay, and you don't brace and you don't leave and you don't throw it, and you let the wave do what waves do.

## The Window

Here's what makes that possible, and why I don't think you can just decide to do it.

You have a window. It's the range of intensity you can stay with before the system flips and you come off the board. For most people, untrained, that's a minute or two of discomfort before the part that wants out gets too loud to ignore. It says *get out of here*. It says *fix this*. It says *I cannot feel this one more second*, and then you reach for something and the wave dumps you.

The window doesn't widen by force. Gritting your teeth is just a sophisticated way of bracing. It widens by staying inside it slightly longer than is comfortable, then backing off, then coming back. That's the same rhythm as the three passes in Module 3, and it's why I keep saying sip rather than gulp.

A client came into this module with a window so narrow that anything past mild irritation tipped him into shutdown. What he needed wasn't a bigger wave, it was a smaller one he could stay on. Ten seconds in his chest, back to the breath, ten more, then twenty, then thirty. He built the window the way you build muscle, which is not by finding the heaviest thing in the room but by coming back to the gym.

And months later the bigger waves were surfable too. Not pleasant. Surfable.

## Allowing, Not Solving

This is the move people get wrong for the longest, and it's the one I care most about in this chapter.

A client I'll call Adam is a fixer. He'd spent his life solving every difficulty he met, including his own emotional life. Therapy, medication, diet, sleep, supplements, systems for managing his anxiety. None of it had touched the ache underneath.

One session he settled enough to find a heavy pit in his stomach. He stayed with it a minute and started to relax into it, and then the part of him that fixes things came online without his noticing. *Okay. How do I solve this?* And within seconds his body tensed back up.

He looked confused and said, *the second I tried to solve it, it tightened again.*

And I said, it doesn't want to be solved. It's asking to be allowed.

That's become a landmark between us. The ache he'd been trying to fix for twenty years wasn't asking to be fixed, and the fixing was the override in a better suit. The move was to do nothing, which for a man built entirely around being effective is not a default skill.

What you'll find, as you train it, is that the moments you most want to solve something are the moments where allowing has the most to give you. Because the fixing impulse is the protector. Sit with it without acting on it and it softens, and the wave underneath gets more room.

## Body Before Story

One more thing, or none of the rest of it works, and it's that you have to leave the story.

When something fires, the mind arrives instantly with an explanation, and the explanation is so fast you don't catch it as one. If you ride the story you aren't surfing the emotion, you're riding the rumination, and the rumination is just plucking the string again. That'll keep you busy for hours.

So go to the body. Where is this living right now, and what does it feel like? Tight, hot, heavy, hollow, sharp. I want the *what* and the *where* from you, not the *why*.

Adam said it best, after he'd been telling me a long story about something that happened on a retreat. I stopped him, and he said, *I don't care about the story. I just want the sensation.* The story wasn't going to surf him. The pit in his stomach would.

And that isn't anti-thinking, it's sequencing. Sensation first and insight after, and the mind comes back online when it has something worth adding.

## What Arrives After

Here's the part I can't make anybody believe until they've felt it once.

If you stay through the whole wave, something shows up on the other side that you couldn't have reasoned your way to. One client calls it his next move and another calls it skillful action. But either way it's the option that wasn't available in the middle, because in the middle of rage or panic your choices are limited to whatever the reaction will permit.

Which is why I keep telling people not to act at the peak, because the wave at its peak is the contract talking and the wave on the other side is you.

## What Matters Is the Return

One thing before you start, because I'd rather say it than have you decide you're failing.

You'll sit down with this and within two minutes you'll have drifted into the email you have to write, and you'll think, *I can't even stay for two minutes.*

But the leaving isn't the failure. Noticing you left and coming back is the practice, and I'm not asking you to build the capacity to stay perfectly, which nobody has. I'm asking you to build the capacity to return.

## Summary

- Emotions are energy in motion. Allowed to move, they finish. Blocked, they stay.
- Resist, distract, discharge. Three doors, none of them out. Staying is the fourth.
- The window widens by sipping rather than gulping.
- Allowing, not solving. The fixing impulse is the protector wearing better clothes.
- Body before story. The story is the rumination. The sensation is the wave.
- Don't act at the peak. The return is the practice, not the staying.

## Action

Four things.

Read **Equanimous**, which walks you through the new daily sit, and begin it, building toward twenty to thirty minutes.

Then read **The Whole Arc in One Session**. It's one client's session end to end, and if anything above stayed abstract, that's where it gets concrete.

Then find one moment in your week where you'd normally reach for the phone or the drink or the defensive sentence, and don't reach. Stay with it thirty seconds longer than is comfortable, then reach if you need to. Those thirty seconds are the whole thing.

Then work the **Surfing** journal, which takes three real moments from your week, one a day. Don't rush it.

And once or twice this module, give twenty minutes to [*Expanding Our Window of Tolerance*](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/j0vt9ks7mg2j0vw5iuupi/Expanding-Our-Window-of-Tolerance.mp3?rlkey=3d141dk3e7ebirgiks2xkglif&st=f1df421f&dl=0), which walks you through toggling between something you love and something that hurts, widening the window a little each pass. Occasionally, not daily. Equanimous is the daily sit and this is the targeted one.
