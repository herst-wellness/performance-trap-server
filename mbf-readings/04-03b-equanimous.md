# Equanimous

*Begin sitting with the Equanimous meditation as soon as you've read the Surfing an Emotion chapter, then daily for the rest of the module. The recordings are on your Module 4 page, and you can play them there or save them to your phone.*

The Equanimous meditation is the sitting practice that pairs with this module's teachings. Where the Surfing journal walks you through the practice on the page with three specific moments, this walks you through the same work as a daily sit, with my voice.

Equanimity is the dual capacity to be both fully in the experience and aware that you're experiencing it. As I describe it in the intro talk: there's a part of you in the river feeling the wave, and at the same time another part sitting by the side noticing the river pass. The practice trains both at once. You stay sensitive enough to actually feel what's happening, and steady enough to remember *this too shall pass* while it's happening.

## How to use the audio

There are three recordings on your Module 4 page. An introductory talk and two different thirty-minute guided meditations. The two thirty-minute versions are not the same meditation at different lengths. They're two distinct practices on the same underlying capacity. Use them in rotation.

**Intro to Equanimous.** A short talk that picks up where Module 3 left off. We were working with the felt tone, the third stage of the sankhara. This talk introduces what happens when felt tone gets ignored over time (the wounding festers and turns into poison in the system) and how riding the emotion as a wave is what allows it to integrate instead. It also reframes the wounding itself as *sacred material*, the teacher that, given its due attention, keeps revealing something new. Listen once before you begin sitting. Come back to it when you need the reminder.

**Equanimous (thirty minutes), version one.** A guided body scan into one specific concern. After settling through the body, you'll be invited to bring to mind something that's been knocking on your door lately, locate it in the body, and meet it as a part. Three questions to the part: *Is there something you're trying to let me know? What concerns it? What does it need from you in the future?* This version is closer to the parts work from Module 2 and the meeting move from Be With. Use it when something specific is alive and asking for attention.

**Equanimous (thirty minutes), version two.** A guided body scan into the overall felt tone, with extended sensation tracking. Starts with progressive breath (lower belly, then upper belly, then floating ribs, then center of chest, with pauses at the top of each inhale). Moves through a full body scan. Then opens out into staying with whatever felt tone is calling attention, watching it move, shift, and change on its own. The instruction is to keep what you're sensing company, without trying to understand it, fix it, or get it to go away. *All things arise, stay for a while, and eventually pass away. Your job is not to get in the way.* This version is closer to pure equanimity practice. Use it when nothing specific is loud and you're working the underlying capacity.

Most weeks you'll alternate between the two versions. Some people find one fits better and use it more often. Both are correct. Different days call for different doorways.

I'd also like you to sit up and meditate for five to ten minutes before falling asleep, in addition to your main practice. Right before sleep is a moment when the day's accumulated charge often surfaces. A short sit there lets the wave move rather than carrying it into sleep with you.

## Two Questions for the Body

When you've made contact with a sensation and you want to invite it to show you what it's holding, I use two questions.

*What are you trying to prevent?*

*What are you worried might happen?*

You don't ask these with your mind. You drop them into the body and wait, and the body answers in its own time, sometimes with a memory, sometimes a sentence, often with the sensation tightening before it loosens. The shift is the answer.

And they're not asking the protector to explain itself. They're giving it permission to show you what it's been carrying. You'll hear both questions in the meditation, and they're worth carrying outside the sit as well.

## How to sit

Find a posture that's steady and comfortable. A chair with your back supported is fine. A cushion on the floor with your legs crossed is fine. Whatever lets you stay alert without strain.

Hands somewhere they can rest. Eyes closed. At the end of the meditation, you'll open them at half mast, letting some light in without leaving the interior experience too quickly. That's a specific move I want you to practice. The transition out matters as much as the sit.

Before you start either meditation, take a minute to notice the quality of mind you're beginning with. Notice the tone of the body, where it feels open and where it feels guarded. We'll come back to that quality at the end of the sit to compare. Sometimes the only way to see what shifted is to mark where you started.

## What to expect

The body scan opening is doing real work, not just settling. As you bring attention to each region (arms, jaw, face, scalp, chest, belly, back, pelvis, legs, feet), you'll notice that each place you direct awareness becomes more sensitive and more open. The initial guarding softens. That softening is what makes the next part of the practice possible.

When the meditation drops you into the concern or the felt tone, the protector will often show up first. The mind speeds up. You go blank. You feel the urge to fix or solve. All of that is the same protector you've been meeting in earlier modules. Notice it. Don't argue with it. Stay with what's actually present.

The instruction across both versions is the same. *Keep your feelings company as they are.* Not to understand them. Not to fix them. Not to get them to go away. Just keep them company while they move through their natural arc.

When the wave gets loud, you have a few options. You can breathe directly into the sensation, sending the breath toward where you feel it most. If the feelings start to disappear or you notice your mind wandering into thoughts, gently redirect the attention back to the body. If something is too intense, back off to the breath or your hands for a few moments. Then come back. Titration applies here as much as it did in Module 3.

## A few things that come up

**The peak.** At the strongest point of a wave, the part of you that wants out gets loud. *I can't sit one more second. I have to fix this. This is going nowhere.* That's the nervous system reaching for the old discharge. Notice the urge. Don't act on it. The peak doesn't last as long as it feels like it will.

**The falling.** After the peak, if you don't bail, there's a softening. Stay through the falling. Don't declare victory and stop paying attention. The falling is where the system is actually updating. The body is learning, in real time, that the wave passed without you having to do anything about it.

**Blank or numb.** Sometimes the body shows up empty. No specific sensation. That's information, not failure. Numbness is one of the protectors. Stay with the edges of it. Notice where it begins and ends. Sometimes the numbness slowly softens into the sensation it was guarding.

**The wave is bigger than your window.** If you're getting overwhelmed, back off. Switch to the breath. Open your eyes. Take a few minutes of straw breath. Come back when the system has settled. Backing off isn't failure. It's the titration rhythm doing its job.

**The wounding as teacher.** This is the reframe from the Intro talk. The thing in you that keeps showing up, the ache, the tightness, the old grief, isn't your shit to deal with. It's sacred material. The teacher that keeps revealing something new if you give it its due. The creative urge, the gifts you have to bring, live in that tenderness. The practice isn't to make the wound go away. It's to keep showing up to it.

## What Matters Is the Return

I want to underline this because almost everyone gets it wrong at first.

You'll sit down, and within a minute or two, you'll notice your mind has drifted. Into the email you have to write. Into the conversation you had this morning. Into the list of things to do later. You'll think: *I'm bad at this. I can't even stay for two minutes.*

What matters isn't doing it right. What matters is the return.

Over and over, we notice where we leave, and we come back. The leaving isn't the failure. The leaving is the practice noticing itself. The return is the practice. Every time you notice you've drifted and come back to the sensation, that *is* the practice. Not what happens before or after. The return itself.

You're not building the capacity to stay perfectly. You're building the capacity to return. Those are different. The first is impossible. The second is what equanimity actually is.

## Two Questions to Drop In

Once you've made contact with a sensation, you can use either of these prompts to invite the protector to show you what it's holding. They're the same questions I use in session, and you'll hear them in the Equanimous meditation too.

*What are you trying to prevent?*

*What are you worried might happen?*

Don't ask them with your mind. Drop them into the body and wait. The body answers in its own time, sometimes with a memory, sometimes with a sentence, sometimes with the sensation shifting before any words arrive. The shift is the answer.

These aren't questions for the protector to explain itself. They're questions that give the protector permission to show you what it's been holding. Stay with whatever shows up, the same way you stay with everything else.

## Throughout your day

The dual capacity the meditation trains is available outside formal sitting. When something fires mid-day, you can practice both at once. One part of you stays in the river and feels the wave. Another part sits on the bank and remembers this is passing. The two together are equanimity.

A one-minute version of the practice: notice the sensation, locate it, stay with it for thirty seconds, let attention rest on breath or hands, come back for another thirty seconds. Let the wave move what it's going to move.

The formal sit builds the capacity. The in-the-moment version cashes it out. Both matter. Both feed each other.
