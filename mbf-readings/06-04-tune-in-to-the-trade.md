# Tune in to the Trade

*This is **S·T** in **STEP**: still, then tune in to the trade. The first two moves of the outer half. The sankhara, once visible, is now the trade made conscious.*

I was on a Zoom call with a senior executive panicking about layoffs. I was playing my part. Calm. Steady. Professional. The guy he could trust when everything around him felt like it was falling apart.

But something was off that day.

My mind felt foggy. My gut twisted every time I shifted in my chair. I tried to look present on the screen. Nod along. Ask engaging questions. I did my best to hold the container like I always did, but inside, something was wearing thin.

He paused, looked at me through the screen, and asked, *Everything all right there?*

He said it casually, but it cut right through me. My body froze. My face tightened. A wave of heat rushed up my throat. *Shit. He sees what's happening. Don't blink. Just smile. Hold it together.*

There was a split second where I thought, *maybe I'll tell him the truth.*

*Fuck no, I'm not all right. My gut hurts like hell. I haven't slept in days. I'm so tired I can't even think straight.*

Then habit took over. *Yeah, just a rough night. All good here.*

He nodded and kept going. The moment passed, but not for me. I watched myself do the thing I always did. Pull my shit together. Smooth it over. Make it easy for everyone else. I didn't even think about it. It was automatic.

That was the trade. In real time. I saw it happen, and I made it anyway.

This chapter is about how to catch that moment before the trade is complete.

## What a Trade Is

The performance trap is the place where you agreed to give up a part of yourself to stay safe, to belong. You learn, usually at a very young age, that to stay connected, you have to give something up. Maybe you give up honesty to keep the peace. Maybe you give up needs to avoid being a burden. Maybe you give up anger or softness because it isn't safe to have it.

**T. Tune In to the Trade isn't about values. It's about the trade.** The wound is the place where you traded your inner truth for outer safety. Tuning in forces that hidden deal into the light so you can stop paying the cost.

Most of us spend our whole lives making these trades without seeing them. The trades feel like life. Like being a responsible adult. Like keeping the peace. Like being a good employee, partner, son, friend. The body knows first. The throat clamps. The shoulders rise. The breath catches. The protector flares with its old reasoning. *You have to. They need this. You can't disappoint them.* And the trade gets made before the mind has time to catch up.

This is the moment of **T**, the second letter of STEP. The capacity that makes it possible is **S. Still**, the steadiness you've been building since Module 1. Without Still, there's no tuning in. The reactive nervous system can't see its own trades. It's too busy making them.

Still is the half-second before the throat clears and the yes comes out. It's not a state you achieve. It's a move you make. You feel the activation. You hold for the count of one breath. The autopilot doesn't get its usual jump. That's the move. Module 1 built the capacity to slow down. Module 3 built the capacity to stay with what fires. Module 4 and 5 built the capacity to ride the wave and meet the wound. All of that arrives in this single pause. One breath, in real time, while the room is still moving.

## Where I Came to This Move

I came to the importance of this step during the darkest period of my life. On the surface, my career was exploding. I was the guy everyone called when the wheels were coming off. But privately, I was collapsing. My gut was wrecked. I wasn't sleeping. I felt like a rotting orange, intact on the outside, but caving in on the inside. I couldn't perform my way out of it. I had to get clear on what was driving it.

That clarity finally arrived in a session with my therapist Randall. I sat across from him and said, *My gut's a wreck. It cramps all the time. I'm wiped out. I can't sleep. Everything's just... honestly? I feel like I'm coming apart. Can you fix me?*

He chuckled. *Alright. Why don't you close your eyes. Take a breath. See what's going on in there.*

I closed my eyes and scanned. It didn't take long. *There's this knot. In my gut. Low down and deep. It just sits there. Like a rock. Like something's been sitting in there for years, and it doesn't want to move.*

He waited. After a while, he asked, *If it could talk, what might it say?*

I shifted in my chair. Looked down. *I don't know. Probably something like... I'm holding it together.* I paused. *I've always been holding it together.*

Then it popped out.

*Before he took his life, Scott was the problem. I was the proof our family wasn't totally fucked up. I was the good kid.*

I exhaled. *His death didn't give me that role. It just made it permanent.*

We weren't just looking at a stomachache. We were looking at a contract I'd signed when I was young. After Scott took his life, my parents were drowning. The unspoken deal was that the family couldn't handle another problem. So I traded my own grief and messiness to become the good kid. I became low-maintenance. I got good grades. I became the proof that our family was still respectable.

The trade was clear: *I will suppress my own needs to ensure you don't crumble. And in exchange, I get to belong.*

That contract was decades old. It had been running every meeting, every relationship, every moment of *I'm fine* I'd ever said. Including the one with the executive on Zoom.

Tuning in is about catching that transaction in the moment.

## The Double Bind

The trade doesn't happen in a vacuum. It happens inside a setup that makes the trade feel inescapable. That setup is the double bind.

A double bind is a structural trap where any choice you can make feels like betrayal. The classic version, named first by Gregory Bateson and his colleagues in the 1950s, came out of family systems work. A child gets two contradictory signals at once. *Come closer.* And also: *don't need me.* No matter which signal the child responds to, the other one gets violated. The child loses either way. The only move that doesn't lose is to not act, to freeze, to disappear. And freezing is its own loss.

My own bind after Scott was structural in the same way. The explicit message was *we love you, we want you to be okay.* The implicit one was *don't add to the chaos, we can't take it, play your part or we'll ship you off to boarding school.* Both signals were live. If I let myself grieve, I violated the implicit rule and risked the only family I had. If I held it in, I violated the explicit one and lost contact with myself. Both choices felt like betrayal. So I froze in the way kids freeze. I disappeared into the role. I became the good kid.

That kind of bind doesn't just get set up once. It gets installed. The nervous system learns that there is no good move. The body remembers. Every analogous moment in adult life, the freeze kicks back in. The trade gets made before the mind sees it.

This is the structural reason your own trade has been so hard to refuse. You weren't weak. You weren't avoiding conflict because you don't care. You were inside a bind. The bind made every option feel like a loss. So the protector kept choosing the loss that hurt least visibly: the loss of yourself.

Most binds were set up early. A parent who needed you to be a certain way and also resented you for being it. A teacher who said *be honest* and punished you when you were. A culture that said *speak up* and shamed the speaking. The original binds taught your nervous system that there was no good move. The body brought that knowing forward into every analogous situation since.

Tuning in to the trade is partly about seeing the bind that's setting it up. Once you see the bind, the bind loses some of its power. You don't have to play the game by its rules anymore. There's almost always a third option, the move the bind told you wasn't available. That third option is usually the truer move.

## In the Moment

When you feel the trade about to happen, the moment itself becomes the practice. Something in you wants to remain connected, but something else knows you're about to lose yourself to maintain that connection. That's the bind making itself felt. The question to ask in that moment is what the old pattern is costing you. What are you about to give up to stay safe, approved of, or in control?

**Try this.** Notice the compromise forming. Ask:

- *What part of me is about to go quiet so the connection stays intact?*
- *What feeling am I about to bury to keep the peace?*
- *What am I about to trade away right now? My honesty? My time? My need? My dignity? My rest?*

Name the trade.

You don't have to act on what you find. You don't have to fix it or solve it or have the perfect response. The naming is the practice. The thing you couldn't see before is now visible. That visibility is what changes the equation. Once you see the trade in the moment, it stops being automatic. Something else becomes possible.

## What This Looks Like in Real Time

Heather, the leader you met in Module 4, was in another one-on-one with the founder. He was pressing her on a hire she'd already declined twice. Same tone, same raised eyebrow, same implied *this isn't what I'd do.*

She felt the clamp at the back of her throat. The same one from her session two months earlier. The body knew this conversation. It had known it her whole life.

Her old move would have been to smile through it. To soften her position. To find the diplomatic phrasing that would let the founder feel agreed with while she filed away the cost.

This time was different. This time she could feel the trade about to happen. She let the breath stretch out and stayed with the clamp for one beat longer than felt comfortable. In that beat, she named the trade silently. *I'm about to give away the judgment he hired me for.* She named the bind. *He's asking me to agree and to disagree at the same time. Whichever one I do, he'll be unhappy about something.* She named what she actually thought.

Then she said it out loud. Plainly. *I hear you. I don't agree. Here's why.*

The outcome of the conversation isn't really the point. Sometimes the founder pushes back hard. Sometimes he agrees right away. What matters is that Heather didn't make the trade. She sat in her car for ten minutes afterward and let her body know she'd kept her word to herself. The clamp had been there. She hadn't folded around it. That was the practice working.

The next time will be slightly easier. The time after that, easier still. Not because the bind goes away. The bind doesn't go away. Because she's building the muscle of seeing it.

## Where This Comes From

A few people whose work sits underneath what this chapter is teaching.

Gregory Bateson and the family systems researchers around him named the double bind in the 1950s, originally trying to understand schizophrenia. Their insight was that the bind isn't a quality of the person caught in it. It's a quality of the relational system the person is caught inside. That reframe matters here. You're not flawed for having been frozen. You were caught in a structural setup.

Edward Tronick's Still Face work, which we touched in Module 1's *Why We Perform* chapter, is the developmental basis for how binds form. The infant in the Still Face Experiment is being asked, by a non-responsive parent, to make sense of an unanswerable contradiction. *I'm here. I'm not here.* The body learns, that early, that there are situations where every move loses. That learning persists.

Viktor Frankl gave us the language for what becomes possible when you can see the bind: *between stimulus and response there is a space, and in that space lies our power to choose, and in our choice lies our freedom and growth.* The space is the moment you tune in to the trade. The choice is the move you make from there.

You don't need to remember these names to do the practice. I'm telling you so you know the practice has weight behind it.

## How You Practice Tuning In

Three places to practice.

The first is in the moment, using the prompts in the section above. Whenever you feel the trade forming, you ask. The asking is the practice. You don't need to act on what you find. You just need to see it.

The second is the cushion. Your daily Gratitude-Choice-Commitment sit asks you, in the Choice movement, *what is one thing I could let go of today that no longer expresses my higher nature?* That question is tune in to the trade in slowed-down form. Every time you sit with it, you build the capacity to ask it in real time.

The third is on the page. After our session, you'll have your heartfelt intentions and your life purpose statement in hand. The Follow-Up Journal walks you through each one and asks: where am I out of alignment with this? Where am I tolerating something this would never agree to? What's the quiet rebellion my body is already trying to start? Those are tune-in-to-the-trade prompts in their fullest form, applied to your own named values.

The combination of the in-the-moment naming, the daily sit, the post-session journal, and the inner compass document you'll receive from me is what the practice looks like going forward. The trade keeps showing up. You keep getting better at seeing it.

Module 7 is the move after the tuning in. **E. Expand Options** opens up the third move the bind told you wasn't available. **P. Practice** is the repetition that makes the third move the new default. Together they're the action half of STEP. For now, your only job is to start seeing the trade when it shows up.
