# Tune in to the Trade

*This is **S·T** in **STEP**: still, then tune in to the trade. The first two moves of the outer half.*

Your heartfelt intentions are named and your life purpose is on a page. Which is what makes this next move possible and also what makes it hurt, because from here you can see exactly what it costs when you hand one of them over.

Here is the last time I did it, as clearly as I can give it to you.

I was on a Zoom call with a senior executive panicking about layoffs. I was playing my part. Calm. Steady. Professional. The guy he could trust when everything around him felt like it was falling apart.

But something was off that day.

My mind was foggy and my gut twisted every time I shifted in my chair. I nodded along and asked engaging questions and held it the way I always did, and inside something was wearing thin.

He paused, looked at me through the screen, and asked, *Everything all right there?*

He said it casually and it cut straight through me. My body froze, my face tightened, heat came up my throat. *He sees it. Don't blink. Smile. Hold it together.*

And there was a split second where I thought about telling him the truth. No, I'm not all right. My gut hurts and I haven't slept in days and I'm too tired to think.

Then habit took over. *Yeah, just a rough night. All good here.*

He nodded and kept going. The moment passed, but not for me. I watched myself do the thing I always do. Pull it together, smooth it over, make it easy for everybody else. It was automatic.

That was the trade, in real time, and I saw it happen and made it anyway.

So this chapter is about catching that moment before the trade is complete.

## What a Trade Is

A trade is the moment you give something of yours away to stay safe or to belong. Honesty, for peace. Your own needs, so as not to be a burden. Anger or softness, because neither one was safe to have in the room you grew up in.

And most of us make them our whole lives without once seeing one, because they feel like life itself. Like being a responsible adult, or a good employee or partner or son. The body knows first: the throat clamps, the shoulders rise, the breath catches, and the protector flares with its old reasoning. *You have to. They need this. You can't disappoint them.* Then it's done, before the mind has caught up.

## Where I Came to This Move

I found this step in the worst stretch of my life. My career was exploding and I was the guy people called when the wheels came off, and privately I was caving in. Gut wrecked, not sleeping, no way to perform my way out.

It landed in a session with Randall. I told him my gut was a wreck and I couldn't sleep and could he fix me. He chuckled and told me to close my eyes and see what was going on in there.

It didn't take long. A knot, low and deep, sitting there like a rock, and it felt like something that had been in there for years and had no intention of moving. He waited a while, and then asked what it might say if it could talk.

*I'm holding it together.* And then: *I've always been holding it together.*

Then this came out.

*Before he took his life, Scott was the problem. I was the proof our family wasn't totally fucked up. I was the good kid.*

I exhaled. *His death didn't give me that role. It just made it permanent.*

We weren't looking at a stomachache. We were looking at a contract I'd signed young. After Scott took his life my parents were drowning, and the unspoken deal was that the family couldn't take another problem. So I traded my own grief and my own messiness to be the good kid. Low-maintenance, good grades, the proof that we were still respectable.

The trade was clear: *I will suppress my own needs to ensure you don't crumble. And in exchange, I get to belong.*

That contract was decades old and it had been running every meeting, every relationship and every *I'm fine* I'd ever said, including the one on that call.

## The Double Bind

The trade doesn't happen in a vacuum. It happens inside a setup that makes the trade feel inescapable. That setup is the double bind.

You met the double bind in Module 1 as the thing that installed the override, and here it's the thing that keeps the trade running. Same mechanism. Two contradictory signals at once, and whichever one you answer you violate the other, so the only move that doesn't lose is not to move, and not moving is its own loss.

My own bind after Scott was structural in exactly that way. The explicit message was *we love you, we want you to be okay,* and the implicit one was *don't add to the chaos, we can't take it, play your part.* Both were live. If I grieved I broke the implicit rule and risked the only family I had, and if I held it in I broke the explicit one and lost contact with myself. Both felt like betrayal, so I froze the way kids freeze and disappeared into the role.

And that's the structural reason your trade has been so hard to refuse. You weren't weak and you weren't avoiding conflict because you don't care. You were inside a bind that made every option a loss, so the protector kept picking the loss that showed least, which was you.

But once you can see the bind, it loses some of its power, because you stop playing by its rules. There's almost always a third option, the one the bind told you wasn't there, and it's usually the truer move.

## In the Moment

When you feel it coming, the moment is the practice. Something wants to stay connected and something else knows you're about to lose yourself doing it, and that's the bind making itself felt. So ask what you're about to give up to stay safe, or approved of, or in control.

**Try this.** Notice the compromise forming, and ask:

- *What part of me is about to go quiet so the connection stays intact?*
- *What feeling am I about to bury to keep the peace?*
- *What am I about to trade away right now? Honesty, time, need, dignity, rest?*

Name the trade.

And you don't have to act on what you find, or fix it, or have the perfect response. The naming is the practice, because the thing you couldn't see is now visible, and that visibility is what changes the equation. Once you see it in the moment it stops being automatic, and something else becomes possible.

## What This Looks Like in Real Time

A client of mine, the leader you met in Module 4, was in another one-on-one with her founder. He was pressing her on a hire she'd already declined twice. Same tone, same raised eyebrow, same implied *this isn't what I'd do.*

She felt the clamp at the back of her throat, the same one from her session two months before. Her body knew this conversation and had known it her whole life.

Her old move would have been to smile through it and find the diplomatic phrasing that let him feel agreed with while she filed away the cost.

This time she could feel it coming. She let the breath stretch and stayed with the clamp one beat longer than was comfortable, and in that beat she named it to herself. *I'm about to give away the judgment he hired me for.* Then she named the bind. *He's asking me to agree and disagree at once, and either way he'll be unhappy about something.*

Then she said it out loud. *I hear you. I don't agree. Here's why.*

What he did next isn't the point. What matters is that Heather didn't make the trade, and she sat in her car afterward letting her body know she'd kept her word to herself. The clamp was there and she didn't fold around it.

The next one is slightly easier and the one after that easier still, and not because the bind goes anywhere, but because she's building the muscle of seeing it.

## Where to Practice

Three places.

In the moment, using the questions above. You feel it forming, you ask, and the asking is the practice.

On the cushion, where the Choice movement of your daily sit asks what you could let go of today that no longer expresses who you are. That's this same move at a quarter speed, and every time you sit with it you get better at asking it while a room is moving.

And on the page afterward, with your intentions and your purpose in front of you. Where am I out of alignment with this? What am I tolerating that this would never agree to?

The trade keeps showing up and you keep getting better at seeing it. Module 7 is what to do about it, and for now the seeing is the whole job.

## Summary

- A trade is the moment, felt in the body before it registers in the mind, when something asks you to hand over your own truth for the contract's promise.
- Tuning in is catching that hidden transaction while it happens, so you can stop paying for it.
- The double bind is what makes the trade feel inescapable. Every choice feels like betrayal, so the protector picks the loss that shows least, which is you.
- Seen, the bind loses power, and there's almost always a third option it told you wasn't there.
- Three places to practice: the moment, the cushion, and the page after our session.
