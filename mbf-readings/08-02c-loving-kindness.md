# Loving-Kindness

*Cadence: Aim for 16 to 30 minutes daily across the two weeks of Module 8. Loving-Kindness is the new option introduced for this module. You also have permission to alternate with any sit from the prior seven modules that's been most alive for you. The bedtime sit from Module 4 continues.*

Loving-Kindness, called *metta* in the Pali tradition, is the practice of deliberately generating warmth and kind regard, first toward yourself, then toward someone you love, then outward through ever-widening circles, eventually including someone difficult, your community, all beings.

It might sound sentimental. It's not. In its serious form, it's a training. The nervous system can be taught to default to warmth the way it can be taught to default to vigilance. Loving-Kindness is one of the oldest and best-tested protocols for that training.

Module 8 introduces it because integration asks for warmth. The work of the prior modules has been deep. Some of it has been hard. The closing weeks are a chance to let the heart soften toward what you've met in yourself, toward the people who've walked alongside you, and toward the world you're returning to with new capacities.

## How to sit

Find a posture that's both steady and comfortable. Upright is helpful but not required.

Begin by noticing the quality of mind you're starting with. Calm and clear, dull and heavy, or jumpy and agitated. Remember this so you can compare it to where you arrive at the end.

Take a few slow breaths into the belly, lengthening the spine, settling.

Bring attention to the area around the heart. Sense the warmth there, or sense its absence. Either is fine.

Bring to mind someone, a person or an animal, living or no longer living, who you love and who you know loves you back unconditionally. Let yourself feel that love. Let yourself receive it back from them, in your mind's eye, as a wish for your wellbeing. *May I be well. May I be happy. May I be free of suffering.*

From there, extend the wish outward. To someone you love. To someone neutral, perhaps a stranger you see often but don't know. To someone difficult, someone who has challenged you. To your community, your country, the wider world. Finally to all beings everywhere. Plants. Animals. Strangers laughing, strangers crying, strangers in labor, strangers dying.

The audio walks you through these circles in sequence.

## What to expect

The first sit might feel artificial. The phrases can sound rote. The warmth might not come. None of that is a failure. The practice works whether or not you feel the warmth in the moment. The nervous system is being shaped by the orientation, not by the intensity of feeling.

Some days, one circle will close down. You'll find that the difficult person is too difficult, or that loving-kindness for yourself is harder than loving-kindness for others. Don't force it. Notice the closing, send warmth toward the closing itself, and keep going at whatever pace you can.

Over the two weeks, you'll likely notice a subtle shift. Encounters that would have triggered defensiveness soften half a step. The internal commentary about others quiets a little. The reflexive harshness you carry toward yourself loses some of its grip.

This isn't a personality change. It's a quiet recalibration. Enough of these, repeated over years, and you become someone whose default response to strangers, including yourself, is a little warmer than it used to be.

## Throughout your day

The most effective application of Loving-Kindness is not the morning sit. It's the silent micro-practice during the day.

When you're in a meeting where someone is being difficult, internally: *may you be well.*

When you're stuck in traffic next to a driver who just cut you off: *may you be free of suffering.*

When you catch your reflection in a window and the inner critic activates: *may I be well.*

These don't have to be heartfelt to count. The doing of them is the practice. The body learns by repetition.
