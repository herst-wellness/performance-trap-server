# Coming Home

*This completes **E** in **SENSE**. Equanimity has two faces: Module 4 taught staying through the wave, and this is the compassion that lets you meet what's there once you've stayed.*

The last chapter said the wound gets honored and met rather than removed, which leaves the question of what meeting it consists of, since you've never done it and neither had I until somebody showed me.

You've named the protector, traced how it formed, and made contact with what's underneath.

So here's my answer. What you found needs a particular kind of relationship, not a technique. It needs the relationship the protector has never once been able to offer it.

I call that compassion, and I mean something narrower by it than the word usually carries.

## What I Mean by It

People use compassion to mean kindness, or gentleness, or being nice to yourself, or some vague spiritual quality you ought to cultivate. That isn't what I'm after.

I mean something structural. It's the contact you give the wounded part that it never got in its original conditions. What was missing back when the wound was forming is the exact thing it's been waiting for since, and that thing is contact. Steady, non-fixing, non-judging, from somebody who isn't going to leave.

And the hard part is that the somebody has to be you. The original conditions can't be replayed. The people who couldn't give the right kind of attention then aren't coming back to give it now, and the grief of that is real. It isn't the end of it, because the part that's been waiting can be met now, by the part of you that has, by this point, the capacity to be the steady one in the room.

The Buddhist name for that available heart underneath the protection is *bodhichitta*. It isn't soft in the sense of weak. It's clear and alive, and I'd say we're born with it, lose contact with it under the conditioning, and get it back slowly doing work like this.

So turn toward your wound with that and it's finally being met by what it needed. Not optimization. Not discipline. Not insight. Meeting.

## Presence Is the Medicine

This is the middle of the chapter and the thing I most often have to say twice.

Sit with your wounded part for the first time and the impulse is to fix it. Soothe it out of pain. Talk it out of its fear. Be impressive at compassion.

But none of that is the medicine. Presence is.

What I mean is being there with what's here without trying to change it. The part is in pain and you don't have to stop the pain, or optimize it, or teach it anything. You have to be there with it, attentively, without leaving, while it does what it's been waiting to do.

A client of mine is a fixer who spent his adult life solving problems, for other people and for himself. When his younger self finally surfaced with me, his first move was to fix that kid. Make him feel better. Reassure him. Solve his fear.

I kept steering him off it. *He doesn't need you to solve him. He needs you to sit with him.* It took weeks to land, because the fixing was trained so deep. And when he could finally just sit there without doing anything, something moved that no amount of strategy had touched. The kid wasn't being met by a manager. He was being met by somebody who wasn't going to leave.

And that's the act of love in this work, as far as I'm concerned. The part of you that was overridden or exiled needs to be met. Not optimized, not disciplined, not argued with, not transcended. Met.

## The Vulnerable Part Is Sacred

Your protector has spent a lifetime treating that part as a problem. Weak, embarrassing, something to hide and harden over. It learned that where the part wasn't safely tolerated, so keeping it out of sight became the job.

And this module reverses that. It isn't a problem. It's where your particular gifts come from. It's the part that knew what the room couldn't tolerate, felt what the room asked you to stop feeling, wanted what the room couldn't give, and it's been holding something true about you that the rest of you was trained to abandon.

I don't think reclaiming it is sentimental and I don't think it's indulgent. The English word *healing* shares a root with *whole*. To heal isn't to remove what hurts. It's to become whole again with what hurts included.

And I mean even the parts that seem most embarrassing. The tender one you decided was too sensitive. The needy one. The angry one you've been sitting on. Each is carrying something the whole of you needs, and my job isn't to perfect them out of existence. It's to make them welcome.

Another man I worked with spent his adult life trying to fix what he called his neediness, which he thought was the worst thing about him. What he came to instead was that it was the residue of a kid who hadn't been met, and that the kid knew something about what care looks like. It didn't disappear when he stopped fighting it. It turned into the thing that lets him spot when somebody else is in need and give them what he never got.

## Asking for Help

A practical note, because I've seen what comes up in this module be bigger than one person can sit with alone.

There will be moments when it's too big for the sit, the journal, or a walk. The pain can flatten you. The protector can flare into overdrive because what's surfacing feels unbearable.

And in those moments, the most overt self-compassion available to you is asking for help.

A lot of us, and especially men, have built a hard ideal of self-reliance and will struggle alone for years believing that needing anybody is weakness. I don't believe that and I don't live it anymore. Everybody I know who changed anything real did it with help. It isn't a solo journey. It's one done in relationship.

I'm fifty-two and I still find this hard. Two years ago I got news that somebody close to me had taken a turn, and I felt it land on my chest in the night. My move my whole life has been to sit up in a meditative posture and breathe and wait it out alone. That night I woke Melissa instead and told her I was having a hard time and asked her to hold me. It felt completely foreign. Her warmth came over me and I fell asleep. What's worth saying is how hard it was to ask, with her inches away, knowing she'd say yes. Some part of me didn't want to wake her, didn't want to bother her, and mostly didn't want to need.

The people who can do this are the ones who see you out of good form and stay anyway. They aren't fixing you. They're offering presence, the same thing you're learning to offer your own wounded part, and it regulates your nervous system and reminds you that what your critic is saying isn't the whole story.

So if you're touching something this module that feels too big, I want you to reach out. To me, to your therapist if you have one, to the friend who has shown you they can sit with hard things. Asking isn't the opposite of doing the work. It's part of it.

## What's on the Other Side

Met with enough compassion over enough time, something specific happens, and I've watched it happen enough to tell you it does. The protector starts to relax.

It doesn't vanish. The critic doesn't go quiet forever, the achiever doesn't stop reaching, the caretaker doesn't stop tending. What changes is that it stops having to work so hard, because it senses in the body that the vulnerable part is being held by somebody other than just it. The vigilance eases. The grip softens. It becomes more like an advisor than a dictator.

Derek Walcott has a poem called *Love After Love* about the moment you greet yourself arriving at your own door, and the stranger you ignored for years for the sake of other people turning into somebody you can sit and eat with. It's short. Find it and read it once, slowly, after you've done the journal. I won't reproduce it here.

## What This Actually Asks

One last frame, and it's the one I'd want you to keep.

This isn't indulgence, it isn't blaming your childhood, and it isn't excavating the past forever. It's a responsibility to the part of you that got left.

You didn't choose the wound. Your protectors weren't your fault. The conditions weren't yours to control. None of that is on you. What is on you, as the adult you've become, is to be the trustworthy presence your younger self needed and didn't get.

Not to fix that kid. To be there, reliably, without the kid having to perform for the contact.

That's what closes the inner half of this work. After this, Modules 6 and 7 turn it outward, because once you've come home you can see the trades you're making in your actual relationships and start renegotiating. You can't do that first. The one who signed has to be back in the room before there's anybody to renegotiate on your behalf.

## Summary

- Compassion here is the steady, non-fixing contact the wounded part never received.
- Presence is the medicine. It doesn't need solving. It needs meeting.
- Reclaiming the vulnerable part is becoming whole, with what hurts included.
- Asking for help is overt self-compassion, and nobody does this alone.
- Met enough, the protector relaxes rather than disappearing.
- You're being asked to become the trustworthy adult your younger self needed.
