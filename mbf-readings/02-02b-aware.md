# Aware

*Begin sitting with the Aware meditation as soon as you've read the Awareness chapter. Daily or near-daily over the rest of the module. The audio files are in your Audio folder, under the **02 Aware** subfolder.*

The Aware meditation is the sitting practice that pairs with this chapter. Where the Awareness Journal walks you through the four moves in writing, this meditation walks you through the same work as a sitting practice, with my voice guiding you.

The meditation builds in two stages. The first stage uses the breath to gather your attention. The second stage shifts into mindful awareness, where you start noticing thoughts, feelings, and sensations as they pass through, labeling them gently without getting pulled into the story.

## How to sit

Find a steady, comfortable posture you can stay in for the length of the meditation. A chair with your back supported is fine. A cushion on the floor with your legs crossed is fine. Whatever lets you stay alert without strain.

Place your hands somewhere they can rest. Eyes closed, or open at half-mast with a soft gaze just in front of you. The point is alertness, not stillness for its own sake.

## What to expect

The first time you do this, the mind will wander a lot. This is the practice. The wandering isn't a failure. The noticing that you've wandered, and the gentle return, is the practice.

You'll start by focusing on the breath. Lower belly, upper belly, ribs, center of the chest. Slow inhalation followed by a slow exhalation. Notice as the breath comes in. Notice when it pauses. Notice as it goes out.

After some minutes of breath focus, the meditation shifts. You'll start noticing thoughts, feelings, and sensations as they arise. When you notice your attention has drifted, you'll silently label it: *drifting*, or *thinking*. Then return to the breath and the body. When you notice a quality in the chest or belly (tight, jumpy, hot, soft), you'll label that too.

The labeling isn't about getting it precisely right. It's about anchoring your attention. The label is a hook back to direct experience.

By the end of the meditation, what you're cultivating is the capacity to notice without being pulled along. The thoughts keep coming. The feelings keep arising. But behind them, the noticing remains. That noticing is what we're calling awareness.

## Throughout your day

Outside of formal sitting, the same move is available to you. Whenever you notice yourself caught in a thought or feeling, you can do a small version of what the meditation teaches. Notice it. Label it gently (*there's anxiety here*, *thinking again*). Come back to the breath for a few moments. The full sitting practice strengthens what you can do in those small moments.
