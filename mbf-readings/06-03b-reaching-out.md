# Reaching Out

*This document walks you through how to invite three people into the Life Purpose process. The form your friends actually fill out is **06.03C What You See In Me**. Start the reach-out today. The interviews need wall-clock time, and the friends' reflections are the highest-leverage piece of the entire module.*

Module 6 asks you to do something a little uncomfortable: reach out to three people who know you well, and ask them what they see in you. The reflection they give you, paired with your own work in your Natural Strengths companion, becomes the raw material I use to write your Life Purpose Statement.

Most people flinch from this ask. That's normal. Stay with it anyway. The mismatch between how you see yourself and how others see you is exactly the point.

## Who to Ask

Choose three people who have actually experienced you in real life, not just through texts or social media but in moments that mattered. Think of people who've seen you in motion: during a tough season, a creative sprint, or in your daily life. They've witnessed how you show up. That might be a close friend, a former coworker, a mentor, a teammate, or someone you've supported in a meaningful way.

Avoid people who mostly know your curated self: distant colleagues, social media acquaintances, or well-meaning relatives stuck in a past version of you. This isn't about appearances. It's about depth, honesty, and reflection.

## The Ask

Reach out to each of your three people individually. You can use this language or your own version of it:

> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm in the middle of a coaching process that's asking me to take a real look at my life, not just where I've been, but what I'm here to give. Part of that involves getting honest reflections from people who actually know me.
>
> You came to mind right away.
>
> I'd love to talk with you for twenty to thirty minutes about the strengths you've seen me bring to life, the moments when I've seemed most like myself, most lit up, most grounded. I'll send you a short form to fill out ahead of time. It takes about fifteen minutes and helps guide the conversation.
>
> Would you be open to helping? If so, I'll send the form your way and we can find a time to connect.
>
> Thanks for considering it. It means a lot.
>
> [Your name]

Once they say yes, send them **06.03C What You See In Me** and ask if they can complete it before your conversation. Schedule a time to talk by phone, video, or in person.

## The Interview

Let this be a conversation, not an interview. You're not collecting data. You're listening for resonance. Let them talk. Stay open. Some questions you might ask:

- *When have you seen me offer something that felt deeply me?*
- *What do I seem to give others without trying?*
- *When do I feel like someone who isn't performing, but participating?*
- *Have you ever seen me light up while doing something I didn't even notice I was doing?*
- *What qualities come through when I'm at ease but fully engaged?*

Take written notes. But more than that, take it in. Let yourself be seen.

## Tips for Receiving Feedback

- Let yourself be surprised.
- Listen for what feels true in your body, not just what sounds impressive.
- Ask for stories when something feels vague.
- If a strength feels unfamiliar or uncomfortable, don't dismiss it. Get curious.
- Remember: they said yes for a reason. You're not asking for compliments. You're inviting them into something meaningful.
