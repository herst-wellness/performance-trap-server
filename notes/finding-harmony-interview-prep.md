## The thesis (from the July prep — this supersedes the "prove it's yogic enough" instinct)

Ashtanga taught me a great deal about attention, discipline, and staying. It also showed me how belonging becomes conditional — how easily we override ourselves to remain inside a community, a lineage, a relationship, an identity. My coaching grew out of learning to recognize and renegotiate those conditions.

The central subject is **the contract of belonging**:

- Internal: *I will ignore what I know, suppress what I feel, and override what I need so I can remain acceptable.*
- Relational: *I will keep being useful, agreeable, accomplished, undemanding, or loyal — and in exchange, you will let me remain.*

This starts in the book's opening: Grandpa's doers vs. schnorrers. Needing anything threatens dignity and membership. Worth and belonging must be earned. Ashtanga didn't invent that contract — it gave it a place to continue under the language of discipline, devotion, and surrender.

## The Ashtanga story is a belonging story

- Went to Mysore at ~20, after Scott's death, life come apart, looking for order in chaos. The practice gave structure; the community gave people to eat with, talk with, practice beside, become somebody among. That belonging was real — don't dismiss it, some of my deepest friendships came from it.
- Over time, the terms surfaced: certification, authorization, series, proximity to the family, loyalty, returning to Mysore, knowing which questions were safe.
- The contrarian pieces ("nothing traditional about the tradition"; aging and adapting the practice) triggered the response that revealed the contract: *you haven't been back recently enough; you lack standing; therefore your experience doesn't count.* The scathing response to the aging piece was the straw that broke the camel's back.
- Telling it on air, without indicting Harmony, Russell, or the community: "I found a community that gave me a profound sense of belonging. Over time I understood the belonging felt conditional. There were things I could question and things I couldn't. When I spoke from my actual experience — especially about aging and adapting — I felt the door close. And that hurt, because I'd already spent much of my life wondering whether I belonged."
- Keep it about the *feeling*, not re-litigating who was right about the aging piece.

## The health breakdown is the hinge

Years of overriding the body — first through yoga, then through work; even coaching became another performance. The body kept communicating; I used practice to override it, until it screamed. The breakdown was the collapse of the old contract: *I keep producing, practicing, holding it together — and in exchange, I belong.* The body could no longer keep its side.

The revision: **What if my body was not the obstacle to my spiritual life? What if it was the part of me that had been telling the truth all along?** What I had called devotion was often disconnection. The old practice said override. The newer practice says stay, listen, move closer. From control to intimacy.

## SENSE and STEP = renegotiating belonging

- **SENSE** renegotiates the contract with yourself: return from the story/performance/strategy to the signals you learned to distrust. Not self-indulgence, not obeying every feeling, not treating the body as infallible — restoring the body's place in the conversation. Self-belonging. The precise skill: *notice the exact moment you leave yourself in order to preserve belonging.*
- **STEP** renegotiates the contract with others. The client double bind: tell the truth and risk rejection, or stay connected by abandoning yourself. STEP is the third option — not "fuck you," not compliance: "This hurts more than I've been admitting. You matter to me, and I don't want to keep doing it this way." / "I want to stay in this relationship, but the current terms require me to disappear."
- Freedom here isn't transcendence; it's a renegotiated relationship. First with self, then with others.

## The teacher question

- People do need help seeing what they can't see alone — the work depends on relationship. But I've repeatedly met teachers who wanted something: obedience, loyalty, money, agreement, surrender, confirmation of their authority. Years and life force given to Pattabhi Jois while remaining largely invisible — one of the great heartaches, and itself a belonging wound.
- My model: the coach helps create the conditions in which the client can hear the teaching already trying to emerge through their life. **The client is the living dharma.** Their body is teaching, their relationships are teaching, even the pattern that keeps hurting them is teaching.
- How it sounds in a session: "Here's what I think I'm seeing. Keep it, throw it away, or refine it." Interpretive, active, willing to challenge — but never trading the client's knowing for my authority.
