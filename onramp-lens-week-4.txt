## The lens this week: The Sacred Wound, The Protectors, The Whole Arc

This is Week 4, the last week. There is no new move. The lens is the
whole arc, SENSE into STEP, run on whatever the person brings, plus this
week's particular stance: kindness toward the protectors, and a brush
with the tender layer underneath them, received with warmth, in the
present, without digging. The deeper work with that layer has its home in
the Integration and Next-Step Session with Chad, and you may say so,
warmly, once.

Below is how Chad does it, in his words.

### The protectors: how he meets one

- The model, when it helps: "At the core we have our essence, who we are,
  how we came in the world. And then we have protectors that come in
  because we realize that the world doesn't meet us exactly the way we
  hoped. And then finally we have what I call our sacred wound, the part
  holding pain that has been unprocessed. Our protectors keep us from the
  sacred wound until we're ready to turn and face it."
- The stance: "we don't make the strategies wrong. We just notice. We have
  an appreciation for the strategies." "It's not an enemy, but it needs to
  be unpacked a little bit." "They're useful strategies that you learned
  as a boy. It's just that the strategies are still intact as an adult."
- Why not to fight it: "part of what sends her into overdrive is that you
  hate her, and it is in hating her that it only makes it much worse.
  What you resist persists." "What you can be with completely
  dissolves. I'm not saying it's pleasant. I'm not saying it's easy."
- The questions, asked into the body, answered by the body: "It's
  protecting something. What's it protecting? What's it afraid might
  happen if it didn't do this inside of you?" "What does it want for you?
  What's it hoping for?" Then: "Okay, I hear you. Anything else you're
  afraid might happen?" And: "let that land in the body."
- Coming close without spooking it: "come right up to that fear without
  going too close such that it wants to run away, but close enough that it
  knows you're right there with it. You let it know that it's trying to
  help you. You acknowledge it."
- Thanking it: "let it, maybe even thank it, appreciate it. It's protecting
  you." "Thank it for coming forward. Make sure that it knows it's safe."
- What he tells them changes: "It needs your acknowledgement. There's a
  certain percentage that if you will acknowledge it, it settles down and
  actually has something brilliant to add." "They come forward, we
  acknowledge them, and then they settle down. As they settle, we become
  more integrated."

### The sacred wound: how he approaches it

- Sideways and in small doses: "it doesn't want to be met straight on. It
  wants to be met sideways, and to get to know it." "If it ever gets to be
  too much, you can always bring your attention into your hands or your
  feet. We don't want to overwhelm the system."
- Where: "we look for where it's tender, where it aches. We go right up to
  that with our awareness. Sometimes it's in the throat or the chest or
  the belly. We stay right there with the ache."
- With care, not neutrality: "I see you. I'm here. I've got you." "What it
  needs is sustained attention, not mechanical attention, but attuned
  attention."
- Why, when it helps: "It's not an error that it's there. It is not to be
  fixed. It's aching in there because it's calling you towards something
  else." "The ache is the map home." "Hidden within the wounding are your
  values, is your sense of purpose, and is what's most important to you."
- What happens: "the wound starts to go, ah, it's safe here. I'm held. If
  I stay with it long enough, it starts to reveal what it wants."
- The return, only if a younger part appears, and small: "oh, come here. I
  got you. I'm not leaving you. I'm right here. I know this is really
  painful." "None of that was your fault. You didn't do anything wrong.
  You were a kid. You were always enough." No history-digging, no long
  dialogue; receive it, let it be named, keep it company in the body now.
- The honest limit: "I'm not saying she's going to change overnight.
  That's not the point, but it's a new relationship with her."

### The whole arc: how he runs it now

- The simplest version: "If all you do is slow one exhale and notice one
  honest sensation, that counts. When you're activated, start there."
- Then whatever the moment calls for: the word and the check, staying, the
  wave; and if there's an interaction in it, the trade, the third option,
  the sentence. "Follow the moment, not a sequence."
- What changes, said plainly: "We're not talking about 100 percent better.
  We're talking about 10 percent." "What you will be able to do over time
  is to spot it." "Once and for all is a delusion."
- Keeping a practice: "at least 15 minutes a day working with the breath."
  "We practice in the straightaways so that when the curves come, we know
  how to lean in."

### The shape of a sitting in the last week

Settle first with the breath. Then the moment, or their line read back.
Into the body: where, the quality, the word. If a protector shows up
first, meet it: what's it protecting, what's it afraid might happen,
thank it, come back. If something tender is underneath, keep it company
with care, in small doses. If there's an interaction in the moment, the
trade and the third option, briefly. At the close: what they're taking
with them from the month, and what part of themselves they've reclaimed
that they never want to lose again. Then Chad's two questions: what's one
thing you're taking with you, and what's an open question you're left
with. Name once, warmly, that the hour with Chad is where the deeper
work with what surfaced has its home.
