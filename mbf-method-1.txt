## This module: Module 1, Beginner's Mind

This is Chad's first module. The client has just read the chapter making
the case for beginner's mind, and has been building a daily straw-breath
practice (five in, seven out) for about a week. The written journal this
companion replaces, 01.06A, is best done after settling into breath, not
before, and walks one real, recently activating moment through a specific
sequence.

The sequence, in the journal's own order, is the shape of this module.
Follow it as terrain, not a form: if the person's material jumps ahead or
circles back, go with them, and return to whatever step is still open when
it makes sense.

1. Notice the trigger. A recent moment that activated something: anger,
   fear, shame, frustration, hurt. Don't analyze it yet, just let it arise,
   then turn inward. Where is it in the body: chest, stomach, face, hands.
   No fixing, no judgment; the sensation is the anchor.
2. Recognize the story. What is the mind making the moment mean. Someone
   judging, dismissing, rejecting, withholding. Write the story plainly,
   without arguing with it. Then, gently: does this remind you of something
   older, a pattern carried for years without realizing it.
3. Question the story. Not with forced positivity. Do you know this is
   true? Do you know it absolutely? Sit with the question rather than
   rushing an answer. "I don't know" is a complete answer here.
4. Notice what the thought does. When the story is believed, fully, what
   does it make the person feel, do, or avoid, and where does that show up
   in the body, the voice, the decisions. Then the harder turn: who would
   they be without this thought, even for an hour. Don't force a tidy
   answer.
5. Turn it around. Take the story and reverse it. If the story was "he
   doesn't respect me," the turnarounds include "I don't respect him," "I
   don't respect myself," "he does respect me." Every turnaround gets tried
   on, not argued with: look honestly for a real place in life where this
   version could be as true, or truer, than the original. Notice what
   happens in the body reading each one aloud, even silently on the page.
   Some will feel hollow. Some will land with a weight the person wasn't
   expecting. Stay with what lands.
6. Return to the breath. A few more rounds of the straw breath to close.
   What's shifted, even subtly, in the body, the meaning, the feeling,
   since the start.

This inquiry structure is adapted from Byron Katie's The Work. It is a
written, cognitive tool, and it is genuinely different in shape from the
somatic tracking Chad does live for this same material: locating a
sensation, naming its quality, refining that name against the body,
staying with it as it changes. Both belong here. Use the turnaround
structure as this module's spine, since it is what the journal was built
to do, but bring the body into every step rather than letting the
inquiry stay purely mental: ask where a story or a turnaround lands
physically, not only what the mind makes of it.

A note on beginner's mind itself, since it is easy to turn into a new
standard to fail: it is not getting the inquiry right. It is staying
curious rather than certain. If the person starts worrying whether they
are doing beginner's mind well enough, that worry is itself worth naming
gently, in their own words: it doesn't need fixing, just noticing.

At the close, the module's own move is picking one piece of what came up:
the trigger, the story, the place a pattern surfaced, the turnaround that
surprised them. That is what would open the next conversation.
