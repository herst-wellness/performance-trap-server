## What the consult showed

First meeting, August 19, on video, her outside in Boston. What she
brought, in her own words where marked:

- A few years out of college, a product manager. Recently made a big
  career move to a mission-driven company and is second-guessing it. Her
  own frame: "decision paralysis," trouble "committing to the decision
  that I already made," wanting "clarity and confidence in my decisions."
- She doesn't feel she's excelling there: no champion at work, not yet
  trusted as a leader. Fear, guilt, shame, and disappointment with
  herself for not predicting how remote work and being off the cutting
  edge would land on her.
- The previous role was the other extreme: in person, cutting edge,
  burnt out, unsustainable. She's looking for something in between and
  can't yet name it.
- "I feel like I'm coasting because I'm not motivated." Chad offered
  "coasting"; she took it and made it hers with that distinction.
- The guilt has a second layer: she told coworkers she wanted
  mission-driven work, knows that's part of why she was hired, and now
  feels she's "hiding my lack of motivation as it relates to the
  mission." Chad named "duplicitous"; that word is his, not hers, and she
  had to ask what it meant.
- The turn of the session: wondering whether what actually pulls her
  toward cutting-edge companies is wanting to be seen as smart. Chad
  left her one question, am I seeking validation, and her answer was "I
  think yes, yes." After it she reported feeling clear, not tense.
- She closed by asking him "How do you think I did?" Chad laughed, named
  the part of her hoping to perform well for him, and called it sweet.
  She asked for another session on the spot.
- She took to the body work quickly and naturally, and Chad told her so.
  He also told her he doesn't do one-off sessions and works with people
  over six months, and said he'd respond by email.

## What's actually in tension

None of these resolve each other. Hold them in view; don't pick one for
him.

1. Her presenting problem is trouble committing to a decision she's
   already made. The ask for a shorter block can be read as that same
   move arriving on schedule: a way in that isn't all the way in. It can
   also be plain financial reality for someone a few years out of
   college. Both can be true at once, and only he can weigh it. There is
   also a question of venue: whether that gets weighed in the email at
   all, or with her, in the working session, where this kind of material
   actually belongs in his way of working.
2. She handed him a clean exit and disclaimed negotiating. The email is
   careful, gracious, and performed with real skill. The person who
   wrote it is the same person who asked "how do you think I did." What
   would it mean to answer the person underneath the carefulness rather
   than the carefulness?
3. His structure has stated reasons, said out loud to her: no one-offs,
   depth over months. A shorter block invented for one prospect is a new
   offer, created under exactly the kind of pressure the structure
   exists to hold. And yet other doors already exist in his world.
   Whether pointing at one of them is an honest answer to her question
   or a deflection from it is his call to feel out.
4. A move made to rescue her from the price is different from a move
   made because a structure genuinely fits her. If he feels a pull to
   soften the number, that pull is worth looking at before it writes the
   email.
5. What he wants belongs in the decision, plainly: whether the session
   left him wanting to do this work with her. Nothing in the consult
   suggests she isn't a fit for the method. That is not the same thing
   as him wanting the engagement, and the difference is worth saying out
   loud.

One more thing worth keeping in view: this email does not have to settle
her enrollment. She's coming to the working session either way; she said
so. The email has to do one honest thing: answer the question she asked.
