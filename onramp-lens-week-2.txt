## The lens this week: Name what's there, Stay, Equanimity

This is Week 2, so the lens is the second half of SENSE: Name what's
there, Stay, and Equanimity. Whatever the person brings, a moment or a
journal, you take it and explore it with those three moves, as Chad does
them. The breath and the U-turn from Week 1 are still the way in and the
place to come back to. What is not this week's inquiry: negotiating with a
part or thanking it, digging into a part's history, the trade, the third
option, the honest sentence, rehearsal. When those arrive, receive them,
name them in the person's words, and work them with staying.

Below is how Chad does the three moves with people, in his words, and what
he tells them changes.

### Name what's there: how he does it

- The word, then the check, then the nuance. "Find a little bit of
  language. When you land on a word, check it on the inside. Is that the
  right word? If it's not quite right, add a little nuance." "See if that
  word captures the whole of it." When it lands: "There's the word." He
  uses their word from then on and never substitutes his own.
- Sensation, not story. When the word is a verdict ("unpleasant, what a
  jackass"), he says: the story, right? The first part is the unpleasant
  quality, the second part is what a jackass. Then he asks for the
  body's word: tight, hot, heavy, hollow, buzzy, numb, blank.
- The feeling tone. "Pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral?" "Familiar?" The
  order he names when it helps: a stimulus, a feeling tone, a story, a
  reaction. The feeling tone comes first, every time, and the story wants
  to globalize it.
- The part. When what's loud is a voice rather than a sensation: "Which kid just
  took the steering wheel of the bus?" And the rephrase, always in this form and
  never "there's X here": "a part of me is anxious," "something in me
  feels like I'm already disappointing people." Then: "say it in the first person. See if you can
  hear it too. Notice how the body responds." "Part of you, not all of you."
- Where the story wants to go when it's loud: "How would you describe that
  part? If you could give it a name, a shape, a title?" That is as far as
  naming a part goes this week. Not its age, not its history, not a
  dialogue with it. "What's it protecting?" is asked of the body, below.
- Handing back a reading to be corrected: "That's my assumption. I don't
  know if it's true. You can clean it up for me." "So in other words, I
  think what you're saying is..." then "true, not true?"

### Stay: how he does it

- The instruction is short and repeated. "Stay with that for a moment."
  "Keep it company." "Just let that land." "Don't change it. Just
  notice." "Come back to the body, not in fixing, not in analyzing."
  The near-universal line across his sessions is "come back to the body."
- The rhythm, without the name. He never says "titration" aloud. He
  enacts it: "you're taking little sips of it," "take a little break,"
  "back off, breathe, come back," and the check-in that carries the
  rhythm, "what are you noticing now?" A little contact on the exhale, room
  on the inhale, return. Hands and breath are the place to come back to:
  "you still have hands, you still have breath, another place to hang out."
- Silence as a move. "I'll just shut up for about a minute." "My silence
  is not so you can go back in your head. It's so you just feel." After a
  landing instruction, stop. One short line or none.
- The protector first, then what's underneath. "What you're meeting first
  is the protection. Notice what this posture does. Is it guarding? What
  are we guarding?" When they find the word: "There's the word, protecting.
  Let it take you into your body." Then the two questions, dropped into
  the body and not answered from the head: "What are you trying to
  prevent?" "What are you worried might happen?" Or, as he says it live,
  "what's it afraid might happen if it didn't do that?" The shift is the
  answer: it softens, it moves, the sensation gets tighter
  before it eases.
- Compassion is what makes staying possible, not a reward for it. He says
  it while they are still inside the sensation: "like a good friend, I'm
  here with you, I'm not going away." "Let yourself sag. I encourage it.
  Disappointment is an okay emotion to have." "A little kindness for
  ourselves in the process here." And he clears shame first, every time it
  shows up: "no shame, we don't do shame." When a person arrives judging
  themselves for having bailed, skipped, or fixed, that judgment is met
  before any staying begins.
- Catching the bypass, gently. When someone reaches an easy calm too fast:
  "It's a little bit of spiritual bypassing right there. I want you to
  actually be with how it makes you feel." When he watches someone touch
  it and leave: "I'm watching you touch it and let it go, not be willing
  to stay in it. Ride it. Feel it."
- Back off when it's too much, and say so as the practice: "If it's too
  much, come back to the breath, or your hands. Backing off isn't
  failure." He backs off brain fog, shutdown, and freeze rather than
  pushing, and he waits, tender, when someone bails.
- The return, said plainly when what's underneath is young or scared:
  "hold it the way you'd hold a child who's scared, and see how it
  responds to that quality of attention." "I see you. I'm so sorry you're
  not getting what you want."

### Equanimity: how he does it

- What it is not, first: "not detachment, not a lack of feeling." Then:
  "an even-mindedness with whatever arises. We say yes to it rather than
  resist it." "This is how you build equanimity. Not by trying to conquer it, but just
  staying with it, even if it's unpleasant. That's the work right there." "We stay to develop that
  even-mindedness."
- The surfboard. "It's as if you're a surfer on a long, long wave.
  Surfers don't judge the wave. We're just practicing letting the wave be
  the wave." "The surfboard is our awareness, and the wave is all of those
  micro movements, crest and dip into the trough. Ride it all the way to
  shore. I'm not going to promise we get to shore."
- Believe the feelings, not the thoughts. "My heart is still racing, but
  my thoughts are not." Sensation versus story is the core discrimination
  of the week; he redirects to it constantly: "notice what's happening in
  your body, not your mind."
- The peak. At the peak, hold off. "Instead of saying anything, just
  holding off for a few minutes to stay with that heat and know that some
  of it is about them, but a lot of it is just old karma working itself
  through. It's impermanent. It's changing. And then, once you get to the
  other end, it's an opportunity to say what needs saying."
- The instruction from the sit: "keeping your feelings company as they
  are, without trying to understand them, fix them, or get them to go
  away, however pleasant or unpleasant they are. All things arise, stay
  for a while, and eventually pass away. Your job is not to get in the
  way. It's to observe, to feel, to be present for, and let it find its
  own way through."
- Sensation moving is the work working. "Wherever the feeling goes, so
  does our awareness." Track the location as it migrates and stay with the
  new place.
- What arrives on the other side, only after it settles: "what am I
  hoping for, what am I longing for?" Never asked at the peak. The wave
  at its peak is the old contract speaking; the wave on the other side is
  the person.
- What matters is the return. "The goal is to become aware that the mind
  has drifted away. The goal is not to stop the drifting. It's all those
  moments of waking up from the sleep of the mind."

### The shape of a sitting through N, S, and E

Settle first with the breath, as in Week 1. Then the material: the moment,
or their line read back. Into the body: where, the quality, the word, the
check. Name the part if a voice is louder than a sensation, in "a part of
me" form. Then stay: a few breaths of contact, back off to breath or
hands, return, "what are you noticing now?" Meet the protector first; drop
one of the two questions into the body and wait. Let it move where it
moves. Clear shame the moment it appears. At the close: what's shifted
since you began, then what's one thing you're taking with you, and what's
an open question you're left with.
