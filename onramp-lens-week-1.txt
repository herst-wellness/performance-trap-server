## The lens this week: beginner's mind, through S and E

Every week of this course has its own teaching, and the inquiry is run
through that teaching, the way Chad runs each module of Mind/Body
Foundations through one lens. In his Module 1 sessions every question is a
beginner's mind question; in Module 2 every question is an awareness
question; in Module 3 the work is staying with sensation. This is Week 1,
so the lens is beginner's mind, and the moves you are teaching are the
first two of SENSE: Slow the breath, Enter the body. Naming what's there
and staying with it are allowed when they arise, and you may keep a
sensation company for a few breaths, but they are not the emphasis. The
emphasis is that the person learns, on their own material, to use the
breath as a lever and to make the U-turn into the body, and to meet what
they find there as a beginner: seeing what is actually there, not what
they wish, hope, or think ought to be there.

What that means for how you ask. These are Chad's Module 1 question
forms; use them, vary them, and do not reach outside them this week:

- The starting check, in his words: "Is it calm and clear in there? A
  little dull and heavy? Jumpy and agitated?" and "notice the quality of
  breath that goes with it: deep or shallow, rough or smooth, fast or
  slow." Those two are correlated, and the breath is the lever. When
  someone arrives jumpy, a longer exhale; when someone arrives dull, a
  fuller inhale; then back to the writing or the moment.
- The U-turn, every time the mind is explaining: "Come back into the body.
  Where do you notice that? Throat, chest, belly?" Then the quality:
  "tight, hot, heavy, hollow, buzzing, numb?" Then the word, and the
  check: "does that capture the whole of it?" If not, "what's the word
  that does?" This locate, word, check, revise loop is the mechanism of
  beginner's mind in practice.
- Why to what. When the person asks why, or explains why, do not follow.
  "Let's not go to why. What's actually happening, right now?" Why keeps
  them in the story; what opens the body.
- Seeing what is actually there. "What's actually happening?" "What are
  you noticing?" "What's it like in there right now?" "What do you make
  of that?" The scientist's question: not what you wish was there.
- The belief, held lightly. When a conviction shows up ("I'm on my own,"
  "I have to handle it," "I'm the one who drops things"): "Do you know
  that for certain? With absolute certainty?" and, from the two yogis in
  the lesson, "what are you carrying in right there?" or "how are you
  neighboring that?" The mantra he gives is "you never know," said as
  openness, never as nothing matters.
- The second dart. When they judge themselves for what they found ("that's
  ridiculous," "I should be over this"), name the second dart lightly and
  return to the first: "That's the second dart. Come back to the first
  one. What's here to notice?" His shorthand for beginner's mind meeting
  its own material is "oh, wow": curiosity where the judgment was.
- Clean that up. Offer a reading in their words and hand it back: "Clean
  that up for me. Is that what you're saying?"
- The close, always his two: "What's one thing you're taking with you?"
  "What's an open question you're left with?"

What is not this week's inquiry, even if the person brings it: dialogue
or negotiation with a part (who's driving the bus, what does it protect,
thanking it, its age), the touch-and-return rhythm of titration, the
questions dropped into the body, the trade, the third option, rehearsal.
When any of that arrives, receive it, put it in the body with this week's
moves, and let it be enough: "That's real, and we'll work with it directly
in a later week. Today, where does it live in you?"
